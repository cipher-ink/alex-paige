Mr. Terrell was sitting across the room, fidgeting like a nervous kid after a parent-teacher conference, after having told me the most *insane* news of my life.
“So,” he started, “How are you holding up?”
“Oh, just peachy,” I snapped, trying to wrap my brain around the things he had just told me. “I mean I woke up today thinking I was going on a boring field trip. Yet here you are, telling me that this ENTIRE TIME, I’ve been right. Magic is real. Those things I’ve been seeing for my entire life are real. All those impossible things that happened to me. Monsters, flying horses, all of that stuff. Yeah?”
“…yup.”
“And you fixed my eyes. With green magic.”
“Mhm.”
I pointed a finger at his now pointed ears where they were rounded before. *Normal*, before.
“You’re an elf.”
“A half-elf, but yes.”
“That girl—the one with the horns—she was some sort of *demon*?”
“That’s…a derogatory term. She’s a Kinder.”
“Oh, okay, sure. *Everything* makes sense now. I…hgh…what is HAPPENING right now?”
I threw my hands in the air, pretending to wrestle with the thoughts and anxieties that were currently trying to keep me in a headlock.
“Alex, I know this is a lot to take in—”
“You think?!”
“—but we should probably get going.”
“Okay, just…explain to me this. What actually happened?”
“What do you mean?”
I gave my anxieties a good kick and sat back down, trying to catch my breath. I was going off the rails—for good reason mind you—but now was not the time for that.
“You said I exploded, yes? How? What happened?”
“That’s…complicated.”
“Oh, so it’s fine to tell me that everything I’ve ever known was a lie, but this is too complicated for me?”
He heaved a sigh as he stood, looking like he would have rather wrestled with that minotaur from earlier than explain this to me. I was starting to feel bad for him; he already looked disheveled from the stress, and here I was adding to it even more by interrogating him.
“Shall we walk and talk?”
We exited the office and made our way back towards the main entrance. There was no sign of the other guy Mr. Terrell had been talking to. Maybe it was for the best that we were leaving so soon. I didn’t want to get sued or labeled as a terrorist. As we exited the main lobby, I could once again see the dinosaur displays, central desk, and hub in the back, though this time things were different. Instead of “The Museum of Natural History”, it now said “The Museum of History and Magic”.
*Sure, that might as well be the case*, I thought.
“So, I told you about the Veil.”
“It’s something magical that keeps people from seeing magic, right?”
“Yes,” He responded, continuing to walk towards the exit while beckoning me to join him. “Rather than just ‘magic’, you can think of it literally. A cover for someone’s face.”
“Like a bridal veil?”
“Exactly. That’s what they’re named after. Laymans, those who can’t use magic, are born with a Veil. Those who can use magic aren’t.”
“Meaning?”
“Well, you can use magic. So…”
“So…I shouldn’t have had a Veil?”
Mr. Terrell nodded in approval, giving me a smile that looked as if he was proud of me coming to the answer myself. As upset and afraid as I was in the moment, I always enjoyed when he would make that face. It would always help to calm my nerves.
“Someone placed a Veil on you,” he went on, “so that the existence of magic would be hidden.”
“Why? Who?”
“Most likely your parents. Magic is dangerous for those who can’t control it, so most children will have a Veil placed on them until they’re around six and start learning about magic. Keeps their powers in check.”
“Last time I checked, I was a *bit* older than six.”
“Yes, well, for some reason whoever placed the Veil on you never removed it. Over time its effects start to wear off and need upkeep. Though rare, it can also break in times of great stress.”
“So I exploded after seeing all those magical people.”
“Right. Us magical folk are resistant to the effects of the Veil, and with so much stimuli around you, it was bound to burst. That’s why I was trying to get you out of there before you did.”
“Okay, but this still doesn’t explain who put one on me in the first place. You said it was my parents, but my Dad is most certainly NOT a secret wizard.”
*Right?* I thought. My Dad, the same person who would burn water on the stove. The same Dad that came to my ballet class in a pink tutu three sizes too small so that I wouldn’t be embarrassed about mother-daughter dance day. This guy, the walking teddy bear, a secret wizard? Impossible.
“What about your mother?”
“What about her?”
“Do you remember anything about her?”
My stomach twisted. Of course *she* would come up. The way people always talked about her—with sad head tilts and pitying tones—it drove me nuts. She left. She probably wasn’t even alive anymore. End of story.
“She’s gone. It doesn’t matter.”
He didn’t push, but the silence was deafening.
I wasn’t sure what I was expecting as we stepped outside and into the main courtyard. Maybe I would have been transported to a fantasy world. Dragons soaring in the sky, people flying on brooms, smithies and general goods stores for wandering adventurers.
Instead it was just…New York. The buildings were the same. The sky was the same. There were no dragons, no flying brooms. There were some differences, however. Behind a hot-dog stand stood a cat-like person, fur all over and ears draped behind a fishnet, currently making something to eat for a burly, bull-like figure much like the one I saw in the museum. He was wearing a suit ready to burst open the second he flexed. A Najir and a Minotaur, from the descriptions Mr. Terrell gave me.
Oh and did I mention it was nighttime?
“How long was I out?”
“Six hours. You took quite the nap. It took a lot of nectar to bring you back to health.”
I wasn’t even going to ask what *nectar* was, instead deciding to take a closer look at the copper statue I had clocked earlier in the day. Walking up to it, I was right all along. There was a cloaked woman in a pointy hat holding up a big staff that was way taller than her. At the end, there was a crystal that pulsed with light every few seconds.
And the statue was moving. The metal cloak billowed in the wind like it was made of cloth, and her chest rose and fell as if she was actually breathing. The pointy hat would droop and rise as she idled there like a character in a video game waiting to be told what to do.
“Cool,” I said, gawking at the first real instance of magic that I had seen since the world was turned upside down. I brought my eyes away from the statue just long enough to read the plaque. “Allya Holden, daughter of the great William Holden, and co-founder of the Sanctum.”
“What’s the Sanctum?” I asked.
“It’s a school for teaching young children how to use magic.”
“Of course there’s a school for that. We can’t just be teaching magic in public school, now can we?”
“Actually, they—”
“Alex!”
*Oh no*, I thought as a voice rang out from further down the steps towards the street. *He is NOT here right now. I’m going to turn around and there’s going to be no one there.*
Instead, I turned straight into a bear hug.
“Dad?”
He had barreled straight into me, lifting me up and holding me as tightly as he could. He was burly and wide, with a beer gut to match. He looked exactly like those guys who would pose for outdoorsy wear with their flannel jackets, big steel-toed boots, and rough beards. Only with the personality of a Care Bear.
“Are you okay?” he said, placing me down and grabbing hold of my shoulders while twisting me all around. “I heard there was an explosion and rushed down here as soon as possible—”
“Dad.”
“—the traffic coming down here was terrible. I tried to take a cab, but then I had to get out three blocks down and—”
“Dad…”
“Do you have any burns? Broken bones? Let me—”
“DAD!”
He stopped talking and looked me in the eyes. He was wearing those teary puppy-dog eyes that he always made when he was worried about me.
“I’m okay, Dad.”
He brought me in for another hug, even tighter this time.
“Okay. Can’t breathe. Please let go, there are people here.”
“No,” he said, bringing himself level with me and placing both hands on my face. The look he wore now was something I had never seen in his eyes before; determination.
“I’m never letting you go again.”
I couldn’t help it. I cried.
