We both bolted down the trail like our lives depended on it—which they did. The path felt narrower going back than it had going up, branches snagging at my hair and my ankle deciding it was the perfect time to start screaming at me like a banshee. Every step felt like someone had swapped my foot out for a chew toy.
“Dad, who were those people?” I huffed, clutching a cramp in my side.
“I don’t know.”
*Very reassuring.*
“Okay, well, where are we going?”
“The school. The Sanctum. It’s the only place you’ll be safe.”
There it was again, the school for magical misfits like myself. It seemed like the universe had already decided that I would be attending, whether I wanted to or not—I did not. When we reached the road, we dove into the car. Dad didn’t even bother turning around—we didn’t have that kind of time—and instead threw it in reverse, flooring it. With one hand on the wheel and his head half out the window, he looked like some reckless action hero from those car movies everyone loves. I never watched them, but they made ten so they must have been good.
I clung to the *oh-crap* handle like it was the only thing keeping me from flying away.
“Watch the front!” he barked, his eyes darting between the rearview and the tree line.
“Right, watch the trees, got it.” I squinted into the darkness ahead, half-hoping, half-dreading to see something.
Then I saw it: a flicker of red, glowing faintly in the distance.
“Fire!”
Sure enough, a ball of flames was being lobbed in our direction. Dad yanked the emergency brake, spinning the car like he’d been training for years to do this move. The fireball hissed passed us, lighting up the forest brighter than a Fourth of July party. Dad didn’t even flinch; he straightened out the car and kept going. Me? I was white-knuckled, holding on for dear life and trying not to lose the non-existent lunch I’d had.
“Sorry, Al,” he muttered, as if I’d be *offended* by his life-saving stunts. “We have to get there asap, and I’m going to need to be a little reckless.”
“How long?” I asked, trying not to sound as freaked out as I felt.
“Twenty minutes.”
*What?*
“That’s it? So the all-powerful magic school is in our cabin’s backyard?”
He winced as he took a hard turn, his shoulder probably causing intense pain.
“It’s the opposite. Your Mom wanted it to be close. For emergencies.”
Classic Mom—planning my entire life while Dad and I lived our oblivious, normal lives. Well, as normal as they could be with malfunctioning soda machines and exploding museums.
The road twisted and turned like it had something to prove, and Dad took the curves as easy as he did playing Mario Kart.
“Dad, when did you learn to drive like a maniac?” I asked, tossing back and forth with each turn.
He smirked—like actually smirking in this situation.
“I was…a bit of a delinquent before I met your Mom.”
“Define ‘delinquent’.”
“Oh, you know—winning street races. Betting and selling cars to fund cruises along the Atlantic.”
He floored it again, the speedometer hitting speeds I don’t even think the car knew that It could do. It was at that moment that I realized how little I had known him. My Dad—the soft, teddy bear, flannel-wearing guy—had been some adrenaline junkie before I was born? And he was telling me this NOW?
“Meeting your mother changes all that. I straightened out. I stopped racing and…other things.”
*I don’t wanna know.*
He pulled another hard turn, not slowing in the slightest. I was about to go flying through the windshield when he instinctively reached out a hand to catch me.
“And then you were born,” he said, “The moment you were born everything changed. I had never thought I wanted a kid. Thought it would be too much of a hassle.”
*That’s wonderful to hear.*
“But then I held you. I looked into your green eyes. You wrapped your tiny little hand around my finger. And I knew then that I would do ANYTHING to protect you.”
“I’m sorry,” he continued, “I’m sorry for hiding all of this from you. I know you’re upset at me, and rightfully so, but you were right. I wanted to protect you. After your Mom disappeared…”
He trailed off into silence as we turned into a straightaway. He slowed down enough to take a breath, turning to me.
“She originally wanted me to bring you to the school if anything happened to her. She knew that something, some people were after her, and they would be after you too.”
The twists kept coming—both on the road and in his stories—every word made me realize just how little I knew.
“But why?”
“She never said. She did her own job of protecting me. And you, of course.”
I felt like I had just been given a puzzle to solve. I was trying to put it all together, but it was like I was only given white pieces with nothing to go off of.
“I brought you here because of that.”
“What do you mean?” I asked.
“Every year, I brought you here with the intention of telling you everything. I was going to let you go to the school where you would be safe, but I couldn’t do it. I couldn’t let you out of my sight.”
I was about to retort before be continued—
“I know that I sent you to all the other schools, but those were different. You were within reach. I could see you. But there…”
He trailed off once again. His face stayed glued to the road—which I appreciated at the speeds we were going—but I watched his expression change. Regret, sadness, grief, regret again. It was a kaleidoscope of all the faces that made my heart ache.
“I was going to move closer to Harmony Hills. So I could see you more often. I planned to surprise you over the summer. But—”
“Now that can’t happen,” I finished.
I didn’t want to admit it, but his words said something to me. Something that he wasn’t saying plainly, but I understood. This school was different from the others. Not just in the way that it was magical, but in terms of him.
He wouldn’t be coming with me.
