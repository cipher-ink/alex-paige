I dreamt that I was in a forest, no—*The* Forest. That one you always see in fantasy movies with elves in the trees, magic in the air, and odd discoveries around every corner. The trees themselves towered over me like skyscrapers, their canopies so thick that only the faintest bit of starlight managed to peek through. The ground was soft and damp, moss crawling over everything like a nice carpet. The air smelled like fresh rain, clean and earthy, with a hint of something…magical. Beautiful, right? Well—until the bushes rustled to my left and a woman leaped out at me.
She bolted out from behind a tree, heading straight for me. My brain screamed *move*, but my feet decided they would rather stay planted like a deer in headlights. Before I could react or reach out my hands to stop them, they passed right through me. THROUGH me. Like I was a ghost. A cold shiver slipped down my spine and I spun around to get a better look at her.
She was beautiful in a chaotic kind of way—silvery gray hair that shimmered like metal in the moonlight, falling messily down to the small of her back. It was also tangled up with twigs, leaves, and all sorts of other foresty things. Her face was soft and pale, except for the terror that was strewn across it. As she looked back towards me I saw piercing green eyes. They were…familiar somehow, and looking right at me.
“Hi,” I awkwardly waved. No response. She wasn’t looking AT me, but THROUGH me.
It was then that I heard noises—angry and desperate voices calling out from the direction she had just come. And this horrible sound, like a dog barking mixed with the rattling of a snake. The woman disappeared behind another tree and was gone with the wind. Not wanting to figure out what sort of monster could make that noise, I followed.
After what felt like forever, I finally caught up to her behind a massive rock. She still didn’t notice me there, even after waving my hand in front of her face a few times. She was scanning the forest, her breath ragged like she’d been running for miles. I followed her gaze and saw something amazing: a valley that belonged in a fairy tale, not real life. Mountains encircled a lush basin, rivers spilling into a crystal-clear lake at its heart. Perched on a hill by the lake was a Gothic castle, spires and all, with one impossibly tall tower that pierced the clouds. Below the hill was a glowing town, lights scattered like colorful stars. Maybe they were having a festival or something.
The woman held out her arm and I noticed an intricate tattoo. It started with a black line at her wrist and developed into intricate patterns of circles and runes. Much like the woman, the tattoo felt familiar for some reason.
Then it started to glow, because of course it would. It gave off this faint burnt-orange light, like it was alive. She spoke into the night, her voice shaky but determined.
“Caylin, it’s me. I’m not sure I’m going to make it back in time for the ship. Just go without me. I’ll figure something out.”
Her face tightened as if she was fighting back tears, but then she smiled—a bright and beautiful smile, even with a tear running down her cheek.
“Tell Alexandra…I’ll see her soon.”
And just like that, my heart stopped.
“Mom?”
The word escaped my mouth before I could stop it, and the second it hit the air, I knew it was true. *This was my mom*. Memories I hadn’t realized were there suddenly resurfaced. Her holding me, stroking my hair, calling me her “Shining Star”. My chest ached. How could I have forgotten her face?
She froze. Her head turned slowly until our eyes locked. She wasn’t looking through me anymore. She was looking at me.
“Alexandra?”
