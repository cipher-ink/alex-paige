You know, finding out your Dad knew about magic this whole time and had been keeping this *massive* secret from you for your entire life—not so bad, right? Yeah, not exactly a Hallmark moment. But I handled it with grace—if by grace you mean a lot of yelling and enough confusion to power a reality TV show.
“Why,” I asked, my voice already shaking. “Why didn’t you tell me?”
I wasn’t angry—yet. Shock had parked itself in the driver’s seat, but fury was in shotgun waiting for the green light.
“Was it Mom? Did she *make* you keep it from me—”
“No, Al,” he interrupted, his voice soft like he was walking on eggshells. And let me tell you, the eggshell treatment just made me *angrier*. “It was me, when your Mom disappeared, I—”
“You what?” My voice climbed higher. “Decided it was for my own good? That I’d be better off not knowing?”
My blood was beginning to boil the more I thought and the more I talked. My fists clenched tight enough to leave marks on my palms.
“Is that why I couldn’t remember Mom? Did you *erase* her? Did you think that would fix everything?”
He opened his mouth to speak, but I wasn’t done. Not by a long shot.
“No! You *don’t* get to talk right now. My life just got nuked from orbit, and you’re here trying to pretend things are normal. Well, things are NOT normal!” The dam broke, and every thought of confusion, every thought of frustration, came pouring out. “You of *all* people should understand what my life has been like. I’ve been seeing things that I couldn’t explain since I was a *kid*. And now I find out that you KNEW all along. That you what? *Ignored* it?”
I was on a roll, barely pausing for breath. Everything was fitting together. It all made sense now, and it wasn’t making things any better.
“All those therapists, the school counselors, all that ‘help’ you sent me to—that was just damage control, wasn’t it? Were they in on it? Doing the ‘upkeep’ on my Veil so that I would stay blissfully ignorant?”
He didn’t respond. He only sat there in shame, staring down at the floor with his hands limp in his lap. And that silence? That silence broke me. The anger gave way to something different; betrayal.
I felt tears burning my eyes. *Great*, I thought, *just what I need right now*.
“All I wanted was a normal life. A life with you. But you sent me away again and again. You kept me at arm’s length because…because—what? Did I remind you too much of Mom?”
His head snapped up to respond, and I felt some twinge of jealousy for a woman I barely even remembered.
“Al, no. It’s not like that.”
“Then what is it, Dad?” My voice cracked, but at this point, I didn’t care. “What was it that made you push me away? Why—why did you leave me?”
“It’s…complicated,” he said, running a hand through his hair and avoiding my gaze. “Your abilities. Your mother’s abilities. They’re rare. Even among your people.”
*My people?* Oh, that did it.
“Dad, YOU are my people! I don’t care about magic! I don’t care about any of it. I want you! That’s what I’ve always wanted. I want to have hot chocolate here by the fire. I want to read books together. I want to come home from school, brush off your ‘How was your day?’, but still have you care enough to ask. I want you to ruin dinner so we have to go out for dessert. I want my Dad.”
The silence that followed was deafening.
Finally, after what felt like a century, he spoke. His voice was quiet and heavy with dread.
“We can’t go back, Al.”
He was wringing his hands like he was trying to get all the guilt out of them.
“Your teacher, Mr. Terrell…he told me. Now that your Veil or whatever is broken, it can’t be repaired.”
“So that’s it?!” the laugh that escaped me was bitter and hollow. “Because I know magic exists, I can’t have a Dad anymore? How is that fair?”
“It’s not about fair, Alex.” His hands clenched as he closed his eyes, talking through gritted teeth. “There are people after your abilities. I—I can’t lose you like I did your mother.”
“Then we can face them together,” I begged. “You and me, Dad.”
He shook his head, finally bringing his eyes up to meet mine. I hated what I saw in them. Resignation. “No, you need to go someplace safe. Somewhere you can learn to use your powers. The school your mother wanted...”
*No. No, no, no. Not again.*
“You’re sending me away again?!” I was shouting now, my hands trembling as they flung about. “After everything I just said? After ripping my heart out and putting it on the table, you’re just…throwing me away?”
“Al, it was your Mom’s—”
“I DON’T CARE ABOUT HER!” The words tore from my mouth in a scream. A light bulb popped in the kitchen. I didn’t care. I was too far gone. “She’s gone! She doesn’t matter! She doesn’t mean anything to me any—”
“*Alexandra Paige.*”
The words hit me like a slap in the face. He never used my full name. *Never*. He didn’t even raise his voice. He was calm and resolute. I froze, the anger snuffed out like a candle in a snowstorm. All that was left was cold and dark.
And I said it. The words that came from deep within. The words I regret saying the most, even to this day.
“You care about her more than you ever did about me.”
His face crumpled, the words cutting deeper than any blade *ever* could. And suddenly, I hated myself. I slapped a hand over my mouth to keep from talking, but it was too late. The damage had been done.
I turned around and ran.
