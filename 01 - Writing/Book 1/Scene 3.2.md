I was running back through the forest towards the cabin, or at least I hoped I was. My sense of direction wasn’t exactly top-tier on a good day—this was *not* a good day. The farther I went, the darker it got, like the forest had decided that I was the enemy. Shadows seemed to creep up the tree trunks. The canopy thickened, swallowing the moonlight and my vision along with it.
Hadn’t Jen said the forest would protect me? Right now it felt like I was about to get ambushed.
I slowed to a stop, staring at a tree that looked suspiciously familiar. I’d definitely passed it before. I turned in a slow circle, scanning the forest. Every tree looked identical, standing like silent sentinels in the gloom. My chest tightened. Was I going in circles? Was the forest keeping me here?
Desperation clawed at my throat. I couldn’t afford to waste time. Dad was at the cabin, and I didn’t know what those people wanted with him—or me—but I wasn’t about to sit around and find out.
“Trust my home.”
Those words echoed in my mind as I stood in the darkness. I wasn’t able to see—maybe I wasn’t supposed to see. She showed me those flowers for a reason, right? I thought back to the warmth I felt; that connection. Could I do that here? It was a long shot, but adrenaline had me by the coattails.
I closed my eyes and inhaled deeply, trying to remember the Glowbelle. The warmth, the flow of energy; It felt like I had been a part of something bigger.
*I need to find my Dad*, I pleaded to the forest.
It wasn’t cooperating. The only response I got was a cold breeze carried through the trees.
I groaned, grabbing a rock from the ground and scratching a mark into the nearest tree for reference. Then I picked a direction and ran.
A minute later, I stumbled right back to where I started. The marked tree was waiting for me like it was Harvey looming around a corner. I slumped against the tree in defeat; something was definitely keeping me here. My breaths came out in sharp puffs, my chest tightening with every second I sat still, unable to find my way back to the cabin. Back to my Dad.
*Sitting here isn’t going to fix anything.*
Leaning back against the rough bark, I closed my eyes once again. This time, I let the surroundings wash the tension away, forcing myself to listen—the faint chirp of crickets, the creak of branches in the breeze, the leaves whispering their secrets above me. Slowly, I began to feel something stir. A faint pulse; a heartbeat. But it wasn’t mine.
There it was again, that feeling of connection. A threat, stretching out from me and into the ground. I placed my hand on the gird, trying to let it flow. It began to spread, like a spider web, weaving into the forest. The tree I was leaning against was connected to the others, their roots tangled together in an intricate, unseen network. I could feel the worms wriggling in the soil, the ants scurrying along the bark.
For a moment, it was beautiful—only for a moment. I was too wired, too focused. My only thoughts at the moment were finding my Dad, and as if responding, my senses began to reach out. They probed at the web-like network, looking for any sign. Like plucking strings and listening to the vibration.
*Ding.*
Something tugged at my back. To my right, far off and moving quickly—a dense, pulsing energy that was unmistakably human-shaped. One of the hooded figures.
As soon as I touched their presence, they froze. My stomach dropped and a gross, crawling, sensation took over, like a centipede rushing up my spine. I could sense them, and they could sense me.
I didn’t wait. I bolted in that direction, angling to circle around them. Even while running I still felt a relative connection; just enough to get a direction. If they were heading towards the cabin, so was I. I only had to get there first. I may have taken karate, but I didn’t think that would help against people who could throw fireballs and lightning.
