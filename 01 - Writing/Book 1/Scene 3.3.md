I broke through the tree line and saw the cabin perched on the edge of the cliff, it’s windows glowing warmly in the night. Relief flooded through me for a second—only a second though. There was a person on the front porch. Small frame. Definitely not Dad.
Cursing under my breath, I ducked back into the trees and started making my way around to the side. I had to get in without being seen, and there was only one other entrance. My eyes landed on the small patio off the main bedroom. No stairs. No gate. Just a five-foot gap between the cliff edge and the railing of the patio. I could make it—maybe.
*Don’t think. Dad needs you.*
I ran again. This time in the right direction.
The person on the patio was focused on the direction I came from, so this was my only option. I sprinted like a cat burglar with zero upper body strength. When I reached the edge, I leaped forward, grabbing hold of the railing and swinging myself over.
*SQEEEAK.*
The railing sagged under my weight, sounding like a dying mouse. My heart stopped as I landed with a wobbly crunch, holding my breath and praying nobody heard that. I glanced towards the porch; nothing. Not even a twitch.
*Thank you, universe.*
I used one of the mismatched patio chairs to steady myself and get to my feet. Four chars and a weathered table sat in a sad little cluster, along with an ashtray I’d never seen my Dad use. Toward the back of the patio was a door leading to the bedroom, next to a window with the curtains closed. I crept over, making mental notes of every board that seemed even a little *creaky*.
The curtains were just too small to cover the windows entirely, which left a small gap at the edge; just enough to peek through. The place was a mess, like a tornado had swept through the bedroom. Drawers open, clothes everywhere, suitcases emptied. Whoever was here was obviously not playing around. Then I spotted Dad. He was slumped in a desk chair tied up with his hands behind his back. And unconscious. His flannel had a dark red stain that was definitely *not* ketchup, and a nasty-looking bruise marred his left cheek. My heart jumped into my throat. I’d run away and let this happen to him. I may not have been able to change the outcome—but that didn’t make it hurt any less.
He was still breathing though. Small wins.
I twisted the doorknob carefully and slithered inside. I hurried over to Dad’s side and crouched down, shaking him by the shoulder.
“Dad,” I whispered. Nothing. I shook harder. “Dad, come on.”
Still nothing. He was out cold. Desperation clawed at the door, and I winced at what I was about to do.
“Sorry,” I whispered, and then slapped him.
His head shot up, wild-eyed and gasping like he was about to yell for Bloody Mary. I slapped a hand over his mouth.
“Shh. Dad, it’s me. It’s Alex.” I hissed. His breathing slowed, and his eyes softened with recognition.
“Al?” he mumbled under my hand. I nodded, moving my hand away.
“Al, what are you doing here?”
“I’m here to rescue you, duh.”
His expression immediately shifted to Dad mode.
“Why are there twigs in your hair?”
“Seriously? *Now?*”
I spun him around in the chair, inspecting the knot that was holding back his arms. It was some complicated sailor thing—cause of course it was—but luckily for me, Dad’s years in the Boy Scouts hadn’t gone to waste. I found the center seam and pulled, the knot fading away as Dad dropped to his knees beside me.
He grabbed me in a bear hug before I could react.
“I’m so sorry, Al.”
For a second, I wanted time to freeze. I wanted to stay in that big warm hug of his. I wanted to imagine that we were going to spend the summer relaxing. But the state of the room and the stains on my Dad’s flannel reminded me that we were *very much still in danger.*
“Dad, we have to get out of here before—” I had opened my stupid mouth.
The door to the main room creaked open. A man stepped into the room, holding a steaming mug. Rugged, blond, scarred—basically if the words “villainous mercenary” were a person. My eyes zoned in on the mug. My *favorite* mug. My orange-and-black Stitch mug with the “I don’t do mornings” slogan.
*How dare he.*
Before the guy could process what he was seeing, Dad was up and moving. He grabbed the man’s head and *smashed* it into the door frame. You know the crunch you hear when you break a piece of celery? Yeah, it sounded like that. My mug hit the ground, shattering and spilling liquid all over the wood floor. That was two things that were broken—that man’s nose *and* my favorite mug. I knew which was more important.
The guy dropped like a sack of bricks. The crash must have been louder than we thought because another figure burst into the main room from outside.
“What’s going on—”
He saw us. Dad: standing over the unconscious body of his—maybe—buddy. Me: mourning the loss of my mug.
“*Flame!*” the man barked, throwing out his arm like some kind of magical gunslinger—I guess I had seen some of those—the runes tattooed on his arm glowing a burnt orange color. A ball of fire shot from his hand, apparating out of nothing and zipping across the room at us. Dad grabbed me by the collar and yanked me down to the ground while he went full navy seal and rolled over to behind the couch. Dad grabbed a small metal statue that was on the nightstand and threw it at the man, who simply sidestepped it.
“Over here, you—” I’m not going to finish what he said since I’ve been told I’m not allowed to teach you how to say those things. You’ll learn them in time.
The man said *flame* once more and brought his hand up. The flame appeared in his hand as he began to swing it down in an arcing motion.
“Dad, above!” I yelled.
It was too late. The flame thinned out and extended to form a whip, wrapping around the top of the couch and hitting Dad in the shoulder. He let out a groan in pain. I stepped out towards him but my path was suddenly blocked by the wood from below me starting to grow. Thick branches and vines came up from the floor to block off the doorway, preventing me from getting through.
“Dad!”
I couldn’t see through the mess of vines, but I could hear it. He was in the kitchen now, having grabbed whatever he could get his hands on and throwing it towards the entrance. We only had so much cutlery, so Dad was going to run out soon, and here I was stuck in the bedroom with no way—
“Alex, you’re so stupid!”
I darted out the back door and onto the patio. Not using any time to rest, I used one of the chairs as extra height to jump over the railing. It was a five-foot gap between the railing and the cliff, but It may as well have been a hundred feet with how long it took. I caught a glimpse of the rocks below—they did not look appealing.
I tucked and rolled as I landed on solid ground, my ankle twisting in a way that It probably shouldn’t have. However, with my adrenaline surging, I ignored it and scrambled back towards the front of the cabin.
The man stood there in the doorway, throwing fire after fire into the house. I didn’t see Dad, but there was only so much time I had while the guy was distracted.
I ran forward.
I wanted to say something witty, like a nice one-liner you’d get in a movie. Or maybe just a cool, calm, catchphrase, but all that came out was:
“AAAAAHHHHH!”
The man turned around, his face twisting into shock as I tackled him with everything I had. We both hit the ground hard, rolling back and forth as he tried to get me off of him. I held on for dear life, but I’ve never been known for my strength. He brought his fists up and connected them to the small of my back, sending all the air from my lungs. He threw me off, tumbling to the side and hitting my back on the end of the love seat. I looked back to see him getting on his feet, his tattoo beginning to glow.
“He never said he needed the Keystone alive. Fla—”
He was cut off by a comically loud PANG!
The guy crumpled, his eyes rolling back into his head. My Dad stood there, cast iron in hand like Babe Ruth hitting a home run at the World Series.
“Nobody touches my daughter.”
