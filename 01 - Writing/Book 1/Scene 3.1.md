Running. That was all I could do. My legs burned, my chest heaved, but I couldn’t stop. I wouldn’t stop. The sound of my Dad’s voice calling my name from the cabin faded into the background as I plunged deeper into the forest. I apologized to him under my breath, but I needed space; clarity. Something I was *sorely* lacking today.
The forest was a symphony of crickets chirping and the crunching of leaves under my sneakers. Moonlight spilled through the canopy, giving me just enough light to navigate without face-planting. Or so I thought. With the hurricane that was raging inside of my head, It was hard to pay attention to the minefield of rocks and twigs at the foot of the forest. I tripped, my toe snagging a particularly rude root, and gravity took care of the rest. I hit the dirt with all the grace of a troll.
*What was that about not face-planting?*
Rolling over to my back, I glared up at the stars peeking through the clearing I had fallen into. They were obnoxiously perfect, glittering like they had nothing better to do. My Dad used to say that I’d reach for the stars as a baby—chubby fingers and all—grabbing at the night sky. I remembered my Mom calling me a “Shining Star”. Now? I felt about as dull as a forgotten coin at the bottom of a drawer—worn, overlooked, and collecting dust.
“Dad…”
The word spilled from my mouth, carrying all the guilt I was trying to shove down. I said things that I shouldn’t have. Things I didn’t really mean. But what was I supposed to do? Pretend like everything was fine? That everything I’d learned today wasn’t a terrifying weight to suddenly plop onto a fifteen year old? I wasn’t sure which emotion would win this tug of war in my chest: anger at him keeping this secret, or guilt over the things I said.
But I wasn’t going to have much time to think about it as a voice broke me out of my pity party.
*Illya?*
Okay, maybe not a voice. It sounded more like leaves in the wind, branches crackling, and crickets chirping. Nature’s symphony came together and spoke to me; *super normal*. I shot upright to my knees, looking around for the source of the sound.
“Who’s there?”
Silence.
Then it came again, an omnipresent voice from the forest.
*You are not Illya?*
“Uh…no,” I replied.
My skin prickled with anticipation as I scanned the tree line. Just like Mr. Terrell had told me, I could see farther than before. He had at least *tried* to explain it to me, but in that Mr. Terrell kind of way.
“Now that your powers have started to manifest, you’re eyes have become photosensitive.” Mr. Terrell had explained.
*Yeah, like that explained anything.*
I guess I was happy for it though, cause I could see hundreds of feet away, even in the darkness. As soon as the tiniest bit of moonlight crept through the canopy, I could see. It was there, along the tree line; a glimpse of something in the shadows. A figure.
“I see you.”, I called out. Maybe *not* the best idea to call out to a would-be assailant in the middle of the forest at night, but what can I say—I never learn.
It stepped out and into the moonlight. You’d think I’d seen it all at this point, but I gasped in reaction. She was…otherworldly. Her skin was a mix of olive and brown in an almost speckled pattern all down her skinny proportions. There were antlers that twisted above her head into a crown of branches. Her hazel eyes were piercing; I watched as they looked me up and down even from across the twenty-foot clearing. Oh, and did I mention she wasn’t wearing anything? Full-on *birthday suit*.
“Ooookay,” I stammered, covering my eyes and looking away. “Hi, nice to meet you. I don’t know wh0—”
Or what, I thought. 
“—you are, but can you please put some clothes on?”
*You look like Illya*, she said in response. “Said” in the most loose tense you can imagine. The voice was still coming from all around me. I took a peek at her, trying to cover her body with my hand, and her lips didn’t move as she continued to speak.
*You’re eyes. Your hair. But you are not her.*
“Yeah, I don’t know who that is. So, clothes then? Or—”
Oh, wait. I figured it out. I mean, there weren’t that many people that I knew with the same eye and hair color combination. I’d just never heard anyone say her name out loud.
“My mom? You’re talking about my mom?”
I watched her look into the sky in contemplation. While being lost in thought, I heard the rustling of leaves and brought my hand away from my face to see that green moss had grown around her body to form a sort of primal clothing. It wasn’t much, mind you, but it was at least enough that I wasn’t embarrassed. This time, her lips moved as she spoke.
“Perhaps.”
I got a closer look at her now that she was covered up, and—yet again—couldn’t believe my eyes. Her skin didn’t just look like bark, it *was* bark. It was made of wood and moss; definitely not human. Her hazel eyes were also slits like a cat, seeming to glow in the moonlight. She was basically if the gardening section at Home Depot was a person.
She seemed gentle enough, but I suddenly felt my muscles tense like I was sensing danger—or reacting to a predator. She responded to my now panicked state by throwing up both hands to form a peace sign with each, giving a bright smile and tilting her head just the tiniest bit.
“I come in peace.”
*Huh?*
*Pht!* I fell back to the floor with a roaring laugh that echoed throughout the forest. A deep, guttural, laugh that I was in desperate need of in the moment. I probably should have been more wary of her, but I was *not* expecting her to say something like that. Any amount of tension or anxiety I was feeling was gone, now replaced by an intense curiosity.
“Who taught you that?”
“Illya,” she responded, tilting her head to the side in what I assumed was confusion, her face having returned to a neutral state. “She told Jen to say that.”
*Of course she did*, I thought. No wonder my Dad fell in love with her. She was gorgeous AND had a sense of humor. The thought quickly turned to another twinge of jealousy before the stick lady—Jen, I think she said—continued.
“Who are you?”
“My name’s Alex,” I said as I got to my feet, holding out a hand to shake it. “Nice to meet you…Jen?”
“Illya called my Jen,” she answered, not reaching to shake my hand and instead starting down at it like she was trying to solve a really hard math problem. I decided to leave myself hanging and pulled my hand away.
“Why are you here?”
“That’s…complicated,” I deflected. I guess it wasn’t that complicated. I yelled at my Dad and ran away, but I wasn’t about to spill my guts to the first person I saw.
“Why are YOU here?” I asked.
“Jen lives here.”
Ah, yes. Twig lady = lives in the forest = perfect sense, *duh* Alex.
Jen moved into the clearing, walking past me and towards the center. I hadn’t paid much attention after face-planting in the dirt, but it should have been impossible to miss—a perfect circle of flowers, smack-dab in the middle of this twenty-foot clearing. This had *magic* written all over it.
She knelt by the flowers and motioned for me to join her. This had been one of the longest days of my life—I certainly did not have the energy to run away—so I saddled up next to her. 
I liked flowers; back in eighth grade, I’d gone through a gardening phase thanks to my homeroom teacher, Mr. Beck. He was the kind of guy who thought teaching kids about plants was the key to saving the world. Big tree hugger, that one.
He never taught us about these.
The petals spiraled towards the stem like a galaxy frozen in bloom, blending purple and white until they reached the center. Oh, and they were glowing. The faint light was oddly warm against the cool night air. As a cloud passed overhead, some of the flowers drooped; their petals curled up like they’d been caught sneaking out after curfew. Then, as the cloud passed, moonlight bathed the flowers, and—poof—they opened up again. It was…amazing.
“What are these?” I asked, my voice barely above a whisper.
Jen tilted her head once again and I could almost hear the gears turning.
“Illya said…Glowbelle. They drink light.”
“They’re beautiful.”
I reached out towards a flower and hesitated. I wasn’t about to destroy a flower in front of a very obviously—what was she? It was something I learned in Mr. Terrell’s class now that I thought about it. A nature girl…made of wood and leaves…
“Dryad!” I whispered to myself.
It was only then that I questioned why I’d learned about these things in a *History* class.
Before my brain went completely off the rails, Jen reached out and took a flower, plucking it from the ground. Guess she wasn’t *that* protective of nature. She smiled, offering it to me as a gift. When I took the flower into my hand, it exploded.
Okay, that’s a bit dramatic, but the moment *felt* dramatic. The galaxy-looking pattern of the flower suddenly went supernova. Its petals unfolded completely into something resembling a tiny lily. The purple completely gave way to a dazzling white. The middle shimmered much more intensely like there was a miniature star held within the petals. It wasn’t just beautiful; I could feel something overflowing from it. *Magic*—it had to be.
A warmth spread through me, giving me that feeling of a cat bathing in the sun. It was inside me—flowing through me. I could feel a connection with the flower, and the more I focused on it the brighter the light became until I felt another connection. And another. It continued until every flower in the small circle was glowing, completely illuminating the clearing. It was so bright that I thought maybe someone up in space could see it.
When the light finally dimmed, Jen was sitting there quietly, a smile on her face.
“You have Mother’s gifts.”
“Gifts? You mean…magic?” I asked. I wasn’t sure whether to be excited or terrified.
“She planted them,” Jen nodded, completely unbothered by the existential bombs she was dropping on me. “Said to protect them. I did not know why—I do now.”
Her smile turned into a look of pride like she’d just solved the world’s hardest puzzle and was enjoying a victory lap.
“How—how do you know my Mom?”
“She saved me. My home,” she gestured to the surrounding forest, “was attacked. Big—black—lizard? Lots of fire.”
“Wait, wait, wait. A dragon. You’re talking about a *dragon*?”
She nodded. She was serious. My mom—the one who left me, yeah—fought off a dragon. Did she wear a wizard hat and robes? Was she yelling incantations? I giggled at the thought. The more I learned about her—honestly—the more I wished that I had known her. But she left, and according to Dad, she had a reason.
“Magic is dangerous,” I said, half to myself. Jen raised an eyebrow at me; the universal sign for “well, duh”.
“Fire, storm, earth, creature—all dangerous,” she said, pointing a finger at me. “You, me, Mother—all dangerous.”
I didn’t have a witty comeback to give, so I just sat there. She wasn’t wrong. I mean, I *did* just blow up a museum. It wasn’t like the idea of “things are only as dangerous as you let them be” was something new. Of course I knew about it; maybe I just needed a reminder. It had been a *very* long day, okay?
It was then that Jen’s demeanor shifted. The delicate antlers that made the crown above her head twisted and thickened into menacing horns. Her eyes turned a sharp, feral yellow. Long front teeth grew from her mouth as she began to snarl like a tiger being cornered; more predator than person. I stumbled backward as a low grow rippled from her throat, like branches scraping together in a storm. I was suddenly afraid she was going to pounce.
But she wasn’t looking at me.
I followed her gaze. Three figures, robed and hooded, were right outside the clearing, making their way towards us. Jen grabbed my arm gently—despite the claws that now replaced her fingernails—and pulled me aside into the brush.
“Is this where the light came from?” one of them said, his tone clipped and commanding.
“Yeah, I saw it. It had to be her.” Another voice replied, their voice higher-pitched and nervous.
They entered the clearing, their robes becoming more clear in the moonlight. They were mostly black, but with red highlights around the edges.
“Search the area,” the leader said, motioning to the other two. “Find the Keystone. She can’t have gotten far!”
*Huh? Keystone? What was that?*
“And you!” he barked.
“Y-yes, sir?” It was a woman’s voice coming from under the other robed figure.
“Head back to the house. She might try to return.”
What house was he talking about? The only thing resembling a house anywhere near here would have been—
*Oh. Oh no.*
Jen tightened her grip on my wrist.
“You must go.” She said.
“Do you know them?”
“No. They know you.”
So yeah—apparently they were after me. Why? Couldn’t tell you even if my life depended on it, which it did. Jen once again squeezed my arm, her features returning to normal as she gave me a soft smile.
“My home will protect you. Trust my home.”
I nodded, having no idea what she was talking about, but there was only one thing on my mind: Dad. I broke away from her grasp and started off.
“Alexandra.”
I turned, skidding to a stop to look back at Jen.
“How do you—”
She smiled again, gentle and warm.
“See you again.”
