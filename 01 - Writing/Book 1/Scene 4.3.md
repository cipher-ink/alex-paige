*BOOM!*
One second, I was staring at my Dad—trying not to cry—and the next a deafening explosion ripped through the air, slamming into me like a freight train. The explosion struck the rock ten feet away, and the shards of stone erupted like shrapnel. The force of the explosion hit me in the chest, stealing the breath from my lungs and flinging me backward like a rag-doll. My ears were ringing so loudly that I couldn’t tell if I was screaming in pain or just imagining it.
I hit the ground hard, my ankle rolling in yet another direction It wasn’t supposed to. I was coughing and choking on the dust that was swirling through the air, but through the haze, I could see Dad. He was sprawled nearby, clutching his shoulder—the wound having opened up again. His face was twisted in pain, but he was already scrambling to his feet, yelling my name. I assumed it was my name because whatever he was saying couldn’t reach past the ringing in my ears.
The warm glow of the portal behind us flickered, sputtering like a candle about to go out. My head was pounding as I tried to focus, my body screaming to just lie down and let this nightmare pass. But then Dad’s hand closed around my arm, yanking me to my feet.
“Alex, are you okay?” he shouted, the voice finally breaking through the high-pitched whine in my ears.
I was decidedly *not* okay, but I nodded, my throat suddenly too dry to speak. His gaze snapped to the edge of the clearing, and I followed it, my heart dropping like a stone.
There he was. The blonde, hooded, man from before—alive, nose broken, glaring, and panting as if he’d just run a marathon. His arm was raised, the tattoo on his left arm glowing a burnt orange.
“Found you,” he snarled.
Dad didn’t hesitate. He shoved me behind him and charged, every inch of his frame radiating a ferocity I’d never seen before. He wasn’t just running—he was barreling forward; a human battering ram. The man’s confidence faltered, his eyes darting for escape.
Dad swung his good arm and drove his fist into the man’s collarbone with a sickening crack. The guy’s expression twisted in pain, but Dad wasn’t finished. He grabbed the man’s neck, twisted his torso, and used the momentum to throw him into the ground with a judo move that was terrifyingly impressive.
The man hit the dirt hard, coughing up red as Dad loomed over him, one massive hand pressing down on his neck. My ankle screamed in protest as I tried to move towards them, collapsing my back on the ground in a useless heap.
“Look out!” I yelled.
The man’s hand darted to his robe and he flung something fine and powdery into Dad’s face. Dad reeled back, coughing and clawing at his eyes. The grip on his opponent’s neck faltered just enough for the man to snap his fingers and utter a word.
*Boom!*
The explosion ripped through the air, smaller but no less violent. The dust reacted as more miniature explosions followed, a chain reaction that sent both of them flying in opposite directions. Dad’s body hit the ground like a stone.
“Dad!” I screamed, ignoring my pain and rushing to him. He was trying to get to his feet, his shirt torn and stained—shoulder still burned and bloody. He looked terrible, but he was alive.
“Al,” he croaked, his voice hoarse. “You need to go. Now.”
“No!” I pleaded, tears burning my cheeks. “Let me help—”
“With what, Al?”
The words hit me like another explosion to my face. He wasn’t being cruel—he was right. I didn’t have anything. No magic, no weapons, no way to help. What was I supposed to do? Throw rocks at him?
“Dad, I…” My voice cracked. I didn’t know what I was trying to say, but whatever it was wouldn’t come out.
“Go!” he snapped, his voice booming.
Tears blurred my vision as I stumbled towards the portal. Every step felt like betrayal. My uselessness weighed down on me with the weight of the sky. I wanted to scream at myself to do something—anything—but all I could do was limp towards the sputtering portal.
“*Hold!*”
My entire body froze. My limbs locked in place as if invisible chains had wrapped around my body. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw the robed man, his tattoo glowing as his hand stretched out towards me. No matter how much I strained, I couldn’t move. Panic swelled, preventing me from breathing.
I had never felt so powerless. Not when kids bullied me at school. Not when I was expelled over things I couldn’t control. This was worse. I was frozen in place, unable to scream or even breathe.
And there was Dad, slamming into the man like a freight train. The spell broke and I stumbled forward, grasping for breath. I turned just in time to see Dad tackle the man to the ground. They rolled, fists flying, but Dad was bigger, stronger, and angrier. He pinned the man down, his good hand once again wrapping around the man’s neck.
“Now, Alex!” he bellowed.
I turned and ran. My ankle burned with every step, sending fresh waves of agony up my leg, but I didn’t stop. The portal shimmered in front of me, flickering weakly. I threw myself into it, the elastic resistance giving way and pulling me through.
On the other side, I stumbled into a wide, circular room bathed in golden light. The air was cooler here, quieter. I didn’t care about any of that. I turned back to the portal only to see something even worse than my nightmares.
Dad was crumpled on the ground, unmoving. The man had grabbed him by the hair and was now dragging him towards the portal. He lifted Dad up with strength that he shouldn’t have had—at least not without magic. It was a totally unfair fight.
“No!” I screamed, heading back into the portal—but I was blocked. My hands slammed against the portal’s surface, but it didn’t budge. It rippled with every hit like water, but It held firm. I pounded harder, screaming until my throat burned.
“Let him go! LET HIM GO!”
The man placed a hand on the portal and reeled back in pain as arcs of lighting met his touch. He dropped my Dad, who fell against the portal’s surface in a heap. He wasn’t getting shocked, but he wasn’t being let through.
“Dad, Dad, please. Please get up.”
“Oi, I know you’re in there.” The man said. His face was even more beaten up last time. Dad had still gotten in a few good punches.
“This your Papa?” he said, a crooked grin twisting across his face. He grabbed Dad by the hair again, lifting him up just enough to see his face.
When I tell you that my blood was beginning to boil—understatement of my life. I banged on the stupid portal, yelling expletive after expletive, every word laced with frustration. The temperature in the room began to rise, the room getting brighter with every hit against the portal and every scream I let out. My fists ached, the skin beginning to split open, but I didn’t stop. I couldn’t stop. Beyond the portal, the man dragged my Dad away like he was luggage. My stomach churned as he turned back to the portal, a grin snaking his way around his face.
“Yellowfield,” he called, his voice dripping with arrogance. “You want him back, you come to us. Though he may not be in one piec—”
Before he could finish, Dad had awoken. He grabbed the man’s arm, yelling a guttural shout as he twisted and brought the man to the ground once more. They wrestled into the dirt, both yelling as Dad got on top of him and slammed his face into the dirt with terrifying force. Once, twice, three times. Until he was no longer moving.
“Dad?” My voice cracked like glass. The heat that surrounded me dimmed, and for a second everything seemed to collapse into silence. I sank to my knees, my breath hitching as tears spilled down my cheeks. My hand slapped over my mouth, trying to hold it all in, but It wasn’t working.
Dad struggled over to the portal, crawling now, his movements slow and heavy. He placed a trembling hand on the portal’s surface, his voice rasp.
“Al. Al, are you there?”
I pressed my hand against the glass on top of his, my fingers shaking.
“I’m here, Dad. I—”
“I don’t know if you can hear me,” he interrupted, his voice barely a whisper. “But I know you’ll be okay.” He tried to smile, but It came out more like a grimace. He was missing a couple of teeth.
“Stop it, Dad.” I snapped at him, tears once again blurring my vision. “This isn’t a Hallmark movie. You need a hospital.”
He let out a sharp chuckle like he’d heard what I said. He probably knew it.
“I love you, Alex. See you later, al—”
The words choked in his throat as a crack of blue light split the air. My heart froze as his eyes widened, then rolled back. He fell to the ground like a puppet that had lost its strings.
“DAD!”
I slammed my hands against the portal once again. Beyond it, I saw the man, barely conscious, his hand outstretched—tattoo glowing.
Something in me broke. The world around me warped with heat and light, my anger spilling over like a dam finally giving way. The room blazed with heat, the light so intense it painted the walls white. I pressed my hands into the portal again, the surface buckling beneath my fingers as if it were molten glass. Cracks formed where my fingers crushed their way in.
I didn’t think. I didn’t care. I grabbed hold and pulled. The sensation was like ripping apart a stubborn seam, the resistance giving way with a horrible groan. The last thing I saw was my Dad—laying there on the ground—before a blinding, overwhelming light and the sound of shattering glass.
Everything went white.
