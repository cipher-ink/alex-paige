“How did you sleep?”
I shuffled out of the hallway and into the main living area, the smell of bacon and eggs having drifted into my room this morning. There was Mr. Terrell, wielding a frying pan like a pro chef on a cooking show. He had an apron that said “Watch out, good lookin’ is cookin’!” on it. Considering how he was normally about as noisy as a shadow, it was obvious he was going all out. Almost like he knew I’d crawl out of my cave today.
“Good, I guess,” I said, rubbing the back of my neck. “A little sore.”
“That’ll happens the first time you use magic.”
I froze mid yawn. “How did you know?”
“Well, Unless you ran a marathon in your sleep…” he chuckled, raising an eyebrow at me. He was clearly enjoying this.
I rolled my eyes and flopped onto a stool at the kitchen island. He slid a plate of food my way—the classic presentation with toast burned just right—and grinned at me like we were back in history class, but this time it was eggs and bacon instead of Napoleon and Griffins. I’d expected him to console me. Or even worse, offer me a hug. But here he was, smiling brightly as if I hadn’t been ignoring him for weeks.
“Magic works like a muscle.” He said, sitting next to me with his own plate. “The more you use it, the stronger it will get. Now lets eat and you can tell me all about it.”
Homework over breakfast; great. But I started talking anyway. Mostly because the bacon was *fantastic*, but also because he seemed genuinely interested. I explained how I’d been reading the books he gave me, how I tried to mimic what happened in the forest, and how I’d eventually felt the magic rushing through me like an electric current. His eyes lit up like a kid on Christmas morning.
“You continue to surprise me, Alex. It’s a shame you weren’t this enthusiastic in my class.”
“I like your class!” I shot back. “I just don’t like history.”
“What about magic?”
I hesitated, choosing to take a bite out of my biscuit to avoid answering. Magic, and all that came with it, had turned my life upside down. How was I *supposed* to feel about it?
Before he could press me, I blurted “Do you know anything about a white void?”
The question made him freeze mid-motion, bacon halfway to his mouth like I’d just slapped him with one of the waffles. He set the bacon down and gave me that parental look that would make kids squirm.
“Was that in one of the books I gave you?” he asked, his tone careful.
“Yes,” I lied, with all the skill of a guilty toddler. I wasn’t about to admit to having a deep-and-meaningful dream with a void-dwelling, questionably real entity.
“Did it mention a name?” he pressed.
“Uh, nope?” I stammered, stabbing at my eggs. “Just something about a void, truth, stuff like that.”
He looked at me like he was trying to read my mind. I really hoped that he couldn’t, but then he choked on a biscuit. No, literally, not metaphorically. There was coughing, wheezing, and a very undignified scramble for water.
“You good?” I asked, wide-eyed.
“Wrong pipe,” he croaked, before launching into a furious kitchen cleanup and avoiding my gaze.
*Very subtle*, I thought.
It didn’t take a genius to figure out that he knew something. I decided that it would be better off leaving it alone since it was obvious the conversation was over. I’d have ask him later.
“Alex,” he called, pulling me from my thoughts. “Your ankle is fine right?”
“Uh, yeah,” I answered.
As part of the meals, he had also been giving me this brownish-golden liquid to drink. It tasted nice—like a milkshake—and it supposedly helped my ankle get better even faster than it normally would have. Like *magic*.
“Good,” he continued. “Go change into something you can move in. We’ve got training to do.”
“Wait, like gym class? Now?”
“No, no. Just some light work.”
It was—*in fact*—NOT light work.
