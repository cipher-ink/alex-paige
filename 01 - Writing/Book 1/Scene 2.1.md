I don’t remember much after whiting out. I groggily woke a few times, but I always slipped back into unconsciousness moments later. It felt like my body was burning from the inside out, but I could faintly remember a conversation:
“This is a mess. How are we going to explain this to the public?”
“I understand, I apologize.” The voice was Mr. Terrell. “We were waiting for her powers to manifest, but we never expected—”
“What does any of that matter? She blew a hole in the Magic in History exhibit!”
I heard that wrong, I know I did, because they were *not* talking about a portion of the museum called “Magic in History”. I saw all of the signs, and there was nothing that said…wait. There was one that I wasn’t able to read. I drifted off again, unable to think about it any further.
When I finally woke, the first thing I noticed was my head pounding like a marching band had decided to use my brain as a drumline. The second thing? I was not in an infirmary. Instead, two chairs had been pushed together and blankets draped over to form a makeshift couch. I was in an office, and a messy one at that. It looked as if someone had raided a filing cabinet in the middle of an earthquake. Papers were everywhere, but it was hard to make out any details. It was like someone had turned the brightness up all the way, everything lacking detail as my eyes tried to adjust to the amount of light.
It was then that I heard a door open:
“I promise the school will help with the restoration of the exhibit, but you can’t seriously be holding her accountable.” It was Mr. Terrell again.
“Of course I can,” the other voice said. It was rough and scratchy, with a thick southern accent.
“It wasn’t her fau—”
A looming silence fell over the room. They had probably noticed that I was awake.
“Alex!”
I saw the blurry figure of Mr. Terrell rushing over to me.
“Mr. Terrell, what’s going on? Where am I?”
“In a whole lot of trouble, miss.” The other person chastised through gritted teeth. He was obviously upset, but at *me*? I’d never even met this man before and he was talking down to me.
“Don’t worry about him, Alex. What do you remember?”
“Um…I remember catching up with the group. You were there, talking about something, and…”
Then it hit. The memories came rushing back into my head; a flood of emotion overwhelmed me. Fear, anxiety, shame, anger, and more that I couldn’t put into words. Mr. Terrell reached out to comfort me and I slapped his hand away, starting to breathe more heavily as I righted myself in the chair and backed away.
“Can you leave us for a moment?” Mr. Terrell asked the manager.
“What? You think I’m going to leave this girl alo—” Something cut him off in the middle of his speech. I don’t know what, but there was a faint green glow that emanated from where Mr. Terrell stood. Without another word, he exited the room and left me alone with Mr. Terrell.
“Alex, I’m not going to hurt you.”
Yeah, okay, sure. That’s what they all say right before they grab you.
“You were angry at me. You yelled.”
“I know, and I’m sorry. I was…surprised. I needed to get you out of there before you…”
“Exploded?”
He tilted his head in confusion, furrowing his brow.
“I overheard some sort of conversation,” I continued. “Something about blowing up a magical history exhibit? But that can’t be right.”
*Right?*
His silence spoke volumes.
