There’s always that strange hush before a storm—the kind where the air goes still, the clouds gather, and the world seems to pause, waiting for something to break. That’s what today felt like. As if the world was holding it’s collective breath, bracing for tomorrow, when everything would change.
Again.
For the past two months, I’d made a habit of climbing up to my favorite spot: a small, flat patch beside the slanted roof of the cabin, just big enough to sit cross-legged. After a hard day spend running, reading, and blowing up light bulbs, it was a relief to wrap myself in a blanket, cradle a mug of hot chocolate, and watch the sky.
Tonight was no exception.
I took a sip from my cocoa—the real kind, thick and rich, not that powdered stuff. Mr. Terrell always made it for me when he caught me zoning out; his silent way of asking if I was okay without risking one of my trademark sarcastic comebacks.
Though honestly…I wasn’t sure. Not about tomorrow. Not about any of it.
I was supposed to have two months—two months of some sense of normalcy before everything changed. Before I was meant to step into this new, daunting chapter, following in my mother’s footsteps. At least, that’s how it was described. But those two months vanished in the blink of an eye. I’d been so caught in in reading, training, more reading, and a small fortune in shattered lightbulbs, that I barely noticed the time slipping away. Strangely, Mr. Terrell never had me actually *cast* magic. It was all just theory, endless notes, and more magical jargon than I’d ever be able to fit into me head. Magic, the Weave, crests and their designs, runes, metals, and pages about something called a Trial.
I missed it already.
I would have traded almost anything for another two months in this quiet cabin, but luck wasn’t on my side.
“The sky’s nice tonight.”
I jumped, nearly spilling cocoa all over my blanket.
“Mother—!” I bit off the rest, turning to glare at Mr. Terrell behind me instead. “Could you *not* sneak up on people during dramatic moments? I was in the middle of a very emotional flashback.”
“You said you wanted to be alone with your thoughts,” he said, shrugging innocently, “You didn’t say anything about me joining them—quietly.”
I shot a look at him over my mug, which was now only half full and a lot less comforting. I never new how to handle him when he matched my sarcasm.
He settled up beside me with the kind of ease that made the roof feel more like a living room than a tripping hazard. Of course, he wouldn’t be worried about falling. He probably had enchanted floating boots or something.
“Knew I’d find you up here,” he said, eyes locked on the stars. “Last night before classes. Big day tomorrow.”
“Yeah, ” I mumbled into my mug. “Huge. Monumental. Life-altering.”
“Terrifying?”
“Bingo.”
He nodded, unsurprised, and let a smirk crawl on his face as a gentle silence began to settle between us. It was never awkward with Mr. Terrell. He had a way of being quiet with you, not just near you—like he was giving you space, but still right within arms reach.
I pulled the blanket tighter. “Do you think I’ll ever get used to this?”
“To what?”
“All of it,” I said, waving my hand at the horizon. “Magic. Spells. Wizards. Cat and lizard people.”
*Weird void monsters offering me tea*, I thought.
He simply shrugged.
“Super reassuring.”
He smiled faintly, still watching the stars. “I was born into this, Alex. Magic has always been a part of my life. Most of your classmates will be the same. It’s a lot, being thrown in like this.”
My cocoa was cold now.
“But you’ve done well,” he continued. “Better than anyone could have expected, considering…”
No need to explain. We’d gone over it all, again and again. I’d vented, raged, and mourned my fair share over these two months. Was I healed? Not even close. But I wasn’t ready to quit either.
“Maybe I’m just good at pretending,” I deflected, attempting to keep the mood light.
He turned to me—not as a teacher, or a coach, but with a look of understanding that I’d only seen a couple of times.
“You’re not pretending, Alex. You’re surviving. There’s a difference.”
I didn’t have a comeback for that. Instead, I looked up at the stars, bright and sharp without the city lights to drown them out. They seemed to listen, shimmering in the dark. I wanted to ask them something.
“Where is she?”
I hadn’t meant to say it aloud, but the words came out nonetheless in barely a whisper.
“You’ll find her,” Mr. Terrell said softly.
Silence stretched between us like taffy. I’d told him about my mom, about wanting to find her, but neither of us had any idea where to start. Just a half-remembered dream of a strange valley. Sadly, there weren’t any signposts labeled “You are here!”.
Mr. Terrell stood, brushing imaginary dust from his pants. “Come on. You’ll want to be rested for tomorrow. The fifth-year syllabus is…intense.”
“Define ‘intense’,” I said, stretching my legs.
“Let’s just say you’ll need another notebook. Or two.”
I groaned in protest, but followed him down from the roof.
Tomorrow, the storm would break, and I would be right in the center.
