The glowing city lights turned into small specs as Dad floored it down the highway. I was pretty sure this was *not* a legal speed to be driving. He didn’t say a word about where we were going—just plopped me in the car and hit the gas. Oh, and we left Mr. Terrell behind for some reason.
Before we left, Dad had this quiet-but-furious argument with Mr. Terrell. It wasn’t his usual mild annoyance either. Furrowed brow, rubbing his temples—the works. I couldn’t hear anything, but knowing Mr. Terrell it was something like, “There was an accident, yadda yadda blah blah blah.” When they finally came back over to me, Mr. Terrell produced from his pocket a small stone carving, handing it over to me. It was…a lion? Eagle? Both? I knew I’d seen it before, but I was too distracted to think about it. I asked what it was for, and he gave me:
“You’ll understand soon enough.”
*Sure, cause my life isn’t cryptic enough as it is.*
I looked over to my Dad who was hyper-focused on not letting us crash while weaving through traffic like it was an Olympic sport. Turns out, my Dad’s a *very* aggressive driver when he wants to be.
“Where are we going?”, I brought up the nerve to ask.
No answer. Just the hum of the engine and the occasional thud of my body hitting the side door when he would swerve back and forth. His face was set in stone—a hard concentration that was putting me on edge.
“Dad, I—”
“The cabin.”
“Huh?”
“We’re going to the cabin,” he said, leaving no room in his tone for discussion. “I packed some clothes for you already.”
*Why the cabin of all places?* The cabin in question was North in the middle of absolute nowhere, Maine. It was Mom’s originally before she…left, but now my Dad was taking care of it. Usually, it would be our summer escape, the one spot where everything felt normal. No mysterious things, no monsters or expulsions. Just sitting on the deck and sipping Dad’s beer while watching the sunset and making s’mores. But judging by his face, that’s not why we were going.
I was exhausted, and the stress of the day caught up to me in a flash. Before I knew it, I was out cold.
My dreams are usually a blur, but this one? I remember this one clearly.
