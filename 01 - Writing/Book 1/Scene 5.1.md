You are surrounded by white. Heat and light still hum through you, coursing from the core of your body out into the emptiness that lay ahead.
Where are you?
You turn your head—looking for anything—but the void offers no answers; an endless expanse of white stretching to infinity. You feel as though you’ve stumbled into an unfinished painting, a canvas waiting for color that never comes. There is no North, no South, nor East or West. The only reason you can sense up or down is the thin black line that rests on the horizon. The eternity of white collapses upon it far in the distance, yet it does not offer clarity.
You are standing on something—at least, you think you are. Below you is the same glaring nothing as everything else, smooth and unyielding like glass beneath your feet. Not even a shadow could find purchase here. You glance down, and vertigo takes hold; your stomach lurches.
*Don’t look down*, your body screams.
As the energy within you settles, the burning in your veins fades, leaving only a cold, prickling shiver. Goosebumps rise along your arms and a tingling sensation snakes down your spine like a centipede with its hundreds of limbs.
The silence is the worst of it. It’s pure. Oppressive. No crickets are chirping. No wind is stirring. Not even the distant hum of machinery fills the void. The only thing you hear, the only thing that’s real, is your own breathing—ragged and uneven—and the slow, deliberate thud of your heart.
You do not belong here.
That much is obvious.
So you walk.
For how long is impossible to tell. Minutes? Hours? Time becomes meaningless here as you continue, a smear of moments stretched too thin. You keep moving, though forward doesn’t seem to exist. It’s all the same—white, endless white.
Your legs give out and you sink to the ground, falling to your back. You stare into the sky—or what you assume is the sky; it’s the same. No matter where you look, no matter where you go, it’s the same.
You realize that you’re forgetting something—something important. The thought itches at the back of your mind, but when you try to grab it, it slips through your fingers and falls beneath your grasp. You squeeze your eyes tight, hoping that in black you will find it, but you only find more cold, empty, void.
Then, something takes hold.
The feeling comes first—a crawling, creeping awareness, like the sensation of being watched from the shadows.
*But there are no shadows here*, you think.
Your entire body shudders violently, a chill burrowing deep into your bones as though a pair of eyes is pressing against you, suffocating you with their weight. Only, no one is there.
“Who’s there?” you call, your voice cracking. The first words you’ve spoken to the void. It echoes into the distance, bouncing back and forth, distorted and thin.

>*Oh, I’m surprised.*

The void answers, its voice everywhere—and nowhere. Layered, but disjointed like being stretched through a long metal tube. And yet it is familiar. This voice is your own. It’s being thrown back at you, though twisted and alien.

>*I did not think you would sense me.*
>*Most cannot.*

You ask the void to identify itself, but the words seem ignorant as you speak them. How does a void speak? Does a void even identity?
Laughter is the only thing that answers—not human laughter, but a sharp, jagged sound like nails scraping across a chalkboard. You know it is laughter only because of its rhythm.
You spin yourself around and around, the sound of your heart hammering against your ribcage, the sensation of eyes on your back only growing stronger.
And then you see it. And it sees you.
It is not a person—not really. The outline is there, vaguely human-shaped, but the rest is wrong. Its form is a void within the void, a swirling black absence that pulls at the edges of reality. The white of the world around you bends and distorts around it, as if being devoured.
Its face—or what should be its face—is worse. There are no eyes, no features at all. Instead, a wide smile floats around its head, a whirlpool of motion that spirals endlessly. The smile moves, twisting in impossible ways—over the top of its head, around the back, and returning where a mouth *should* be, but only for a moment. Its smile is unnaturally wide and sharp.
It tilts its head at you, legs crossed casually, as though you’ve interrupted it in the middle of something trivial. The smile only grows wider across its face as it slips down and around the arm before coming back up.
*What are you?*
You think, but you realize that your thoughts are not private, said aloud through the void as though you’d spoken them yourself.
It cackles, the sound high-pitched and grating. When it speaks, the mouth does not open, like the creature does not know how it should work. It simply swirls across its body as the words echo throughout the void.

>*That is fairly rude of you. You’re the one invading MY space.*

You apologize, admitting through your thoughts that you are unaware of how you arrived in this void. Your fear is like pulp on your taste buds, thick and unsettling.

>*I would imagine. Would you like some tea?*

It gestures lazily, a hand-like outline moving through the air. You blink. In an instant, there is a table between you, low to the ground, with a battered, green, tea set. The color is jarring, an explosion of something you had almost forgotten within this void’s sterile expanse. Steam curls from the spout of the kettle, as if this creature had been waiting for you the entire time.
Who are you to say no?
Though your body screams at you to run, where is there to run to? Every nerve is alight, a primal instinct urging you to get as far away from the creature as possible. But you know—deep within the marrow of your bones—there is no escape. Not here. This creature *is* the void. You are its guest—its prey.
You take a tentative step forward, lowering yourself to sit opposite the creature. Your body trembles each time the creature moves. Its limbs bend in ways that you cannot fathom. When you think you have an understanding of it’s form, it twists once more to correct your false thoughts. It reaches out to take hold of the kettle which twists in its grasp as though resisting its touch.
It pours you a cup of tea.
This may not have been the best time to mention that you didn’t like tea, but your thoughts are still not your own. The creature does not reply.
The liquid is vibrant, green, and unnaturally clear; leaves are suspended within like tiny fish in a crystalline lake.
You drink, a soft thanks given.
The tea is warm, soothing, and surprisingly pleasant—much better than what your Dad made from the store. You take another sip, savoring the flavor and the warmth given within this cold void.
Your Dad. The thought slips through your mind and into the void like a whisper, an echo from somewhere far away. You reach out to grasp it, but—

>*You’re a weird one, Alex.*

It knows your name. Your heart skips a beat as the question of how explodes from your brain.

>*I know many things. This is my space, after all.*

Its space. It nods its head at you, sipping its tea with an elegance that clashes with the distorted picture before you. It does not drink with its mouth. Instead, the cup bends and twists where its mouth would have been, the tea disappearing into its void. The mouth has made its way down to the chest, slipping around the back. It reaches out a hand, which grasps something from the white—a sugar cube—as naturally as retrieving something from a pocket. It drops three into its tea.

>*Does this place bother you? It’s…quality?*

Yes, you admit. Even if you *could* lie to this entity, you are keen enough to realize that would not be a good idea. Its grin stretches across its entire body now, curling into a squiggle, its non-existent eyebrows metaphorically raised.

>*Good.*
>*It’s so rare for me to have visitors. Much less ones that can see me.*
>*You must be quite the anomaly.*

You hesitate, but your thoughts betray you once again.

>*Who am I?*
>*Yes, an excellent question, but not the one I am interested in.*
>*Alex…why do you fight?*

You are left even more confused than before as it leans backward, disappearing under the table. You crane your neck to see; it’s gone.
You do not hear it, but the hairs on your neck force you to turn around. It is there, behind you, circling you as it speaks, toying with its prey. It chuckles, a low, reverberating sound that coils in the pit of your stomach.

>*There are many reasons, Alexandra.*
>*Power. Love. Family. Legacy.*
>*Boredom.*
>*What is it that drives you?*

>*You do not know, of course. Most don’t when they arrive.*
>*I shall give you a hint: You wouldn’t be here if you did not have an answer.*

You bite back a sharp retort, pulling back the reigns on your thoughts as tightly as you can. You try to piece together your shattered memory. Anger. Pain. A shadow of your father. These are the things you can see, the things you know. But there is something you’re forgetting. Something that is important.
The creature’s presence smothers your thoughts in a thick, choking fog.
You did not bring yourself here, you think.

>*Oh, Alex.*
>*No one finds Truth without reason.*

Your thoughts beat you to the punch. It is not giving you any answers. You set your teacup down, the warmth having long faded and overridden with the sense of urgency to leave.

>*I am not an answerer, Alex.*
>*Would you prefer I give you a prophecy?*
>*Would you like to hear that you are destined to save the world? That you are the chosen one?*

Disdain drips from its words, delighted at the thought of toying with its prey.

>*No, nothing so grand for you.*
>*You are special, child, that much is true.*
>*You will do many things. Some great, some not.*
>*I see it all. The Truth. Your Truth.*
>*And I cannot wait for you to find the answer.*

Your frustration with the entity gives was to confusion as the air shifts around you, the white around you growing dim.

>*You are about to wake.*

You hear the echo of a voice that isn’t your own, that isn’t this entity. It is something—someone—calling your name. Someone familiar. It is telling you to wake up. Was this all a dream? The creature stands to its full height, towering above you. Its body unravels into shadows and jagged edges. 

>*Was it?*
>*You’ll find out soon enough.*
>*I leave you with my blessing, and a message.*
>*Three pillars, Alex.*
>*Fire, ever burning.*
>*Spirit, never resting.*
>*Shield, unyielding.*
>*They will help you on your journey.*
>*Remember them well.*

The words reverberate in your mind as the echo of your name begins to rise, deafening, crashing against your senses. You grip your ears, but it is of no use. The last thing you see is the impossibly large grin of the creature. The last thing you hear is your own shriveled voice cutting through the chaos:

>*I hope you enjoyed the tea.*

You wake up.
