I jolted awake to Dad shaking my shoulders.
“Hey, Al, we’re here.”
I wiped a tear off my cheek only to realize another one was sneaking down the other side. *Great, crying in my sleep*. I quickly swatted the tears away, hoping Dad didn’t notice.
I climbed out of the car, grabbing the backpack he’d so thoughtfully packed for me. I didn’t even want to know what sort of drab old combination of clothes he had managed to scrape together. Probably some hideout relics from middle school. Crop-tops and all. Better than nothing though.
The moon hung high in the sky, lighting our way as we walked the narrow path to the cabin. Crickets were chirping, and the wind whispered through the trees like it was trying to keep a secret from me. It was peaceful, sure, but I couldn’t shake this knot beginning to form in my stomach. We weren’t here for a getaway, and the forest knew it.
The cabin came into view, perched atop a hill near the cliff’s edge. It looked the same as always—small, a little worn, but cozy in its own way. The bonfire pit was out front, surrounded by a mismatched collection of chairs. That was our thing: no two things were alike. Mine was easy to spot. I could almost feel the smooth wood under my hand, the way the left armrest had a screw sticking out that gave me tetanus once. Fun times.
The cabin was a single-story building, but we had found just enough room to make a loft under the sloped roof. You couldn’t stand up, but as a kid, I’d loved climbing up there with my books. No technology allowed—Dad’s rule—so reading became my thing. I could remember us lying up there, reading and talking until the fireplace downstairs turned to embers.
Inside was homely. The living room had a couch, love seat, fireplace, and a tiny coffee table stacked with board games we never played. The kitchen was barely more than a bar with a stove and a few cabinets. There was a solar-powered freezer chest out back instead of a fridge. Above the kitchen, the loft looked just as I’d left it: beanbag chair, blankets, and an unbeatable view of the lake at night.
While Dad unloaded in the bedroom, I dropped my bag on the couch and flopped into the love seat, letting the warmth of the fire he started creep into my soul. Even though it was almost summer, the breeze off the lake made the cabin nice and chilly during nighttime. For a moment, It felt like a vacation. Only for a moment, though.
“Why are we here, Dad?” I asked, breaking the silence as he came back out of the bedroom.
He didn’t even pause for thought.
“How about we get some hot chocolate going? We could—”
“Dad, please.”
That got his attention. He turned to me, and for the first time, I noticed how tired he looked. Bags under his eyes from overworking himself and scratches on the side of his neck from being worried about me. I didn’t want to press, but he couldn’t dodge this. Not tonight.
“I don’t want s’mores. I don’t want hot chocolate. I don’t even want to sleep right now. I want *you*, Dad.”
Something cracked inside me, and everything came spilling out. I told him again about all the weird things in my life like I had a hundred times. About Harmony Hills, Mr. Terrell, the museum, the explosion—about magic. Everything came pouring out like a broken dam. I didn’t care if he believed me. I didn’t care if the Veil made it so that he was just hearing trumpet noises. I just needed him to *hear* it. When I was finished, my chest felt ten pounds lighter.
He was quiet for a moment, just staring at me with his unreadable face. Then he spoke.
“When I first met your mother…”
I blinked. That was *not* what I was expecting.
“…it was love at first sight. I know, it’s cliche, but it’s true. We were on a trans-Atlantic cruise, heading back to the States.”
I stared at him, dumbfounded, as he clutched a pillow, running his hands over the frills like a schoolgirl lost in a memory of their favorite boy-band.
“I was with my girlfriend at the time,” he admitted, “I know. Not my proudest moment. But I couldn’t help it. I was walking down some stairs when I tripped, drinks about to go flying. But then your Mom came swooping in, catching me in one arm and the drinks in another without a single drop spilled. She looked down at me, dipped in her arms, and said ‘Aren’t I supposed to be in *your* arms?’. I could only stare at her. The way her hair gleamed in the moonlight. Her green eyes. How could I not fall in love.”
After remembering things about my Mom and seeing her in that dream, I understood where he was coming from. The last thing I wanted to do was compliment her, but even with twigs in her hair, she was gorgeous.
Dad stood and grabbed a photo off the mantle—one I’d somehow never seen. Mom and Dad smiling together. My heart ached.
“We dated for some time before I got the courage to propose. I had it all planned out—dinner on the pier, a romantic walk, a night on the sea. And when I went to pull out the ring…I slipped and fell overboard.”
I snorted. *That’s my Dad.*
“She dove after me,” he continued, “I was so distraught about the ring, but she just smiled and pulled out one of her own. Of course she would be the one to propose.”
He sat back down, the smile having now long faded from his eyes. They had turned to pain.
“Before we could get married, though, she needed to tell me something.”
No.
“Something about her.”
My stomach started playing twister. I didn’t want to hear this. Not now. Not ever.
“She told me about magic.”
*Crap.*
