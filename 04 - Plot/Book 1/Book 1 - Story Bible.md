# 📖 Book 1 — The Red Sanctum — Story Bible

_Alex Paige Trilogy_

_A living reference document. Update it as the story evolves — this is the single source of truth for continuity._

---

## 1. Premise & Core Concept

- **Logline:** After Alex's life is plunged into a world of magic she never knew existed, she must navigate a school that hates her, a conspiracy that wants to use her, and the terrifying possibility that the family she's desperate to find was never lost — it was stolen.
- **Genre / subgenre:** New Adult / academy fantasy, mystery-conspiracy backbone.[^1]
- **Comparable titles:** _Not yet established._[^2]
- **Central dramatic question:** Will Alex find the family she's searching for, or become the exact weapon the people who took them are counting on her to be?[^3]
- **Themes:**
    - **Found Family** — What Alex thinks she's missing vs. what she actually finds
    - **Belonging vs. Worth** — The Sanctum's hierarchy forces the question; Alex must decide if she believes what the Rings say about her
    - **Identity Under Pressure** — Who is Alex when the world keeps trying to define her (Weed, Keystone, daughter, orphan)
    - **The Cost of Secrets** — Nearly every adult in Book 1 is hiding something, and those secrets have consequences
- **Tone & mood:** Wry, sarcastic first-person voice — self-deprecating, pop-culture-flavored, quick to deflect with a joke — layered over genuinely dark stakes: grief, institutional cruelty, conspiracy, betrayal. The humor doesn't undercut the stakes; it's how Alex copes with them.

---

## 2. Point of View, Tense & Style

- **POV type:** First person, single POV — Alex Paige. Book 1's drafted manuscript is committed to this; multi-POV (e.g. alternating with a future love interest) remains an open question for later books.[^4]
- **Tense:** Past tense.
- **Narrative voice notes:** Sarcastic and self-aware, with a direct-address frame in the Chapter 1 opening ("If you want to live your life in blissful ignorance, here's your cue to exit"). Heavy use of figurative/sensory language ("a grayscale hellhole," "that Charlie Brown trumpet noise"), pop-culture asides, and jokes that double as emotional deflection.
- **Things to avoid stylistically:** _Not yet established._[^5]
- **Sample paragraph** _(from Chapter 1, "I Destroy the Museum of Natural History")_:
    > Look: I didn't wake up this morning thinking _"You know what sounds fun? Blowing a chunk out of the Museum of Natural History!"_, but here we are. Chaos and I are kind of on a first-name basis.

### [[Alex Paige]]

- **Role in story:** Protagonist and narrative driving force — she pushes toward her goal while simultaneously being pushed against by the conspiracy. She and her mother are both required for the Children of Cain's plan, making her her own McGuffin.
- **Age / birthdate:** 15. Born June 6 — the summer solstice.
- **Physical description:** Hair silver/dull gray, cropped at the shoulders, messy — cannot be dyed. Her dad says it "shines like silver"; she doesn't see it, and it's the only physical trace she has of her mother. Eyes green ("seaweed green"). Light freckles, inherited from her dad's side (would have been red hair if not for the gray overriding it). No birthmarks, scars, or tattoos — surprising given how much happens to her. Right-handed. Voice is higher and "brash," more trumpet than flute.
- **Manifestation / Powers:** Controls the density and frequency of light within a spherical radius centered on herself. **Red Shift** (lowered frequency): heat, cold, electronic interference, bending light to near-invisibility. **Blue Shift** (raised frequency): flashing/flashbang effects, ionization, laser-cutting. **Dual Shift** (rapid alternation): disorientation fields, warped time perception, shockwaves. **Photon/Gravity Pressure** (extreme density): supercompression into a miniature supernova, a short-lived gravity hole, directional pressure blasts. Crest spells: **Slip** (reduces kinetic friction), **Grip** (increases it), **Echo** (delayed illusory afterimage). Crest is on her inner forearm (wrist to midpoint), Gothic/Renaissance geometric design, root at the wrist — recently re-encoded with new linework by a modern encoder.[^7]
- **Personality:** Sarcastic, fights with words rather than fists, deflects with humor when she doesn't want to talk about something. Dresses in hoodies and loose clothing; hates uniforms for feeling tight and suffocating.
- **Want vs. need:** **Want** — to find her mother, live a normal life, and acquire the power to make both possible. **Need** — family and security: people she can trust unconditionally who won't leave her like everyone else has.
- **Backstory:** Bounced through a string of private schools after repeated expulsions; most recently six months at Harmony Hills Academy, a non-magical boarding school in Rhode Island, where Mr. Terrell was — unknown to her at the time — already her history teacher.[^8] Her one unambiguous good memory of closeness with her dad is their summers at the cabin.
- **Speech pattern / voice:** Sarcastic narrator, uses "Yike" (singular) as a verbalized curse-substitute, tends toward pop-culture comparisons and self-aware asides.
- **Key relationships:** [[Mr. Terrell]] — mentor/father-figure; [[Professor Thorne]] — mentor turned betrayer; [[Trina]] and [[Wyll]] — found family; [[Tristan Orm]] — rival turning into trust.
- **Arc:** Starts isolated, distrustful, and convinced she has to do everything alone → ends Book 1 with a found family, a truce with Tristan, and the resolve to get stronger because she "wasn't ready this time."
- **First appearance:** Chapter 1, "I Destroy the Museum of Natural History" (Scene 1.1) — first-person narration of the field-trip incident that breaks the Veil for her.
- **Status tracker:** Alive. End of Book 1: at the Sanctum, uneasy truce with Tristan, aware her mother may be alive and held rather than lost, warned by the Children of Cain via Amara.

### [[Mr. Terrell]]

- **Role in story:** The Chiron of the story. Alex's homeroom teacher, the Sanctum's archivist, and her primary mentor and semi-father figure after losing her dad. He introduces her to magical customs and gives her a model of what a magi can be.
- **Age / birthdate:** Looks early twenties; actual age not established.
- **Physical description:** Hazel eyes, shoulder-length brown hair, a frame that "looked like a stiff breeze might knock him over." Voice is calm and soothing — most of his class tunes him out, but Alex finds herself listening despite herself.
- **Personality — outwardly:** A mild-mannered teacher quietly digitizing ancient texts — easy to overlook, easy to underestimate.
- **Personality — actually:** His archival access includes classified and restricted tomes, and he has read all of it. He knows more about Truth and the cosmological foundations of magic than almost anyone at the Sanctum — not because he sought it out, but because the institution handed him the keys assuming he was furniture.
- **Want vs. need:** **Want** — to preserve ancient magi customs and help Alex find her footing, but underneath that, to finally act at the scale he's always known he was capable of; he's spent his career documenting other people's greatness and quietly believes he'll never achieve any of his own. **Need** — to matter in a way that outlasts him. Alex is the first person who actually needs what he knows — she is simultaneously his purpose and his shot at a legacy.
- **Backstory:** Before the Sanctum's events, he was already teaching history at Harmony Hills Academy — Alex's non-magical boarding school — for at least the six months she was there. This isn't referenced in his Sanctum-era characterization anywhere else, which means either it's a coincidence worth resolving or he was already positioned near her before Chapter 1 begins.[^8]
- **Speech pattern / voice:** Calm, soothing, almost melodic — described as a quality that "seemingly only [Alex] could hear."
- **Key relationships:** [[Alex Paige]] — mentee and de facto ward.
- **His tension:** He holds knowledge that could help Alex enormously, but revealing it would expose exactly how much he knows and raise uncomfortable questions about why he's stayed silent. He is genuinely on her side — but he's not fully transparent, and those aren't the same thing.
- **First appearance:** Established pre-Book 1 at Harmony Hills Academy (per manuscript); formally introduced in his Sanctum role early in Act 1/2.
- **Status tracker:** Alive, active mentor role through the end of Book 1.
- **Notes:**

### [[Professor Thorne]]

- **Role in story:** Secondary mentor, trusted ally, and then betrayer — the convergence point of nearly every thread of the school mystery. He wasn't behind what happened before Alex arrived, but at the Sanctum he is connected to all of it, being used as an instrument by the Children of Cain. He also serves as the primary catalyst for Wyll's development through his research and thesis work.
- **Physical description:** _Not established._
- **Manifestation / Powers:** A cursed manifestation tied to an ancestor — described as a "vampire" affliction that slowly corrupts him toward a monstrous transformation. Being managed (poorly) with a drug supplied by the Children of Cain through [[Amara Sol]].
- **Want vs. need:** **Want (early)** — to cure or rid himself of the cursed manifestation, alone, since revealing it would defame his family further. **By Act 4**, the corruption and the Children's influence have warped this into a straightforward hunger for power — he is, by his own trajectory, lost in the sauce. **Need** — stability and a confidant, someone who knows what he is and stays anyway. He never finds this, and its absence is what makes him breakable. By the end, what he needs most is to be laid to rest — and on some level he knows it.
- **Backstory:** The Trial he steers Alex toward is tied to his own ancestor — a "vampire" incident thousands of years in the school's past where the ancestor, undone by the same manifestation curse, was outed and cost the Thorne family their standing among the noble lines (why Thorne himself is, in the modern day, a "third-string" graduate rather than a First or Second Ring product). He was approached by [[Amara Sol]] on behalf of the Children of Cain, given the stolen **Book of Trials** and the truth of his heritage, and offered a way to potentially control his manifestation — in exchange for getting close to Alex, feeding information to the Children, and keeping her unharmed until they were ready to take her.[^9] He manipulated the Trial's selection specifically because it's a perfect recreation of the historical moment he needs answers from.
- **Key beat to track:** The moment in Act 4 where Thorne shifts from someone being used to someone who has chosen. This transition needs to land clearly — it's the line between victim and antagonist. In Act 4, after failing to replicate the Trial's cure on himself, he ambushes and abducts Alex to extract what she knows; her blood accelerates his mutation rather than helping him, and she escapes.
- **His function:** A dark mirror to Alex. They want the same things at the start — relief from isolation, someone to trust, a way through. Alex finds her found family. Thorne finds the Children of Cain. The divergence is the tragedy.
- **Key relationships:** [[Amara Sol]] — handler; [[Wyll]] — inherits his thesis after his fall; [[Alex Paige]] — mentee, then abductor/victim.
- **Status tracker:** Silenced by [[Amara Sol]] / the Children of Cain at the end of Act 4, before he can reveal what he knows.
- **Notes:**

### [[Trina]]

- **Role in story:** Secondary protagonist and the group's "tech specialist" — the Brains to Alex and Tristan's Brawn. She bridges the gap between raw magic and engineered solutions.
- **Physical description:** _Not established._
- **Full name:** Tatiana — "Trina" is a nickname; she resents the name itself, feeling it doesn't suit her.[^10]
- **Manifestation / Powers:** None established — her magic is channeled entirely through magi-tech rather than an innate manifestation, which reads as a deliberate character choice rather than a gap.
- **Personality — outwardly:** Confident, outspoken, always with an answer. The person in the room who seems most sure of herself.
- **Personality — actually:** The confidence is a farce. She trusts her magi-tech implicitly because it's programmable — she knows exactly what it will do. People aren't programmable. Magic isn't predictable. Trina gravitates toward systems because systems don't let you down the way people do. She's also bracing for the Trial as an inevitability she won't be strong enough for.
- **Want vs. need:** **Want** — to complete a specific magi-tech device, not as a project but as a promise to herself: someone she loves is going to die and she doesn't have the time or means to save them, so she's building something that prevents anyone else from ever being that helpless again. She is mourning in advance and engineering is how she copes. **Need** — to trust herself the way she trusts her devices; to act without a guaranteed outcome and discover she was capable all along.
- **Trilogy function:** Her arc is learning that she is the system she keeps underestimating. The device is the throughline — its completion tracks her growth across all three books; Book 1 only gets partial progress.
- **Key relationships:** [[Alex Paige]], [[Wyll]] — found family.
- **Status tracker:** Alive, device incomplete at end of Book 1.
- **Notes:**

### [[Wyll]]

- **Role in story:** Secondary protagonist and the group's reluctant healer — though he won't know that until Book 2. He is Alex's evidence that magic is something that can be feared rather than embraced. The Cowardly Lion of the found family.
- **Physical description:** _Not established._
- **Manifestation / Powers — Black Flame:** Dominion over the Concept of vitality — the animating force in all living and non-living things. Doesn't burn in the traditional sense; it consumes vitality continuously, feeding on everything around it until nothing is left. Cannot be extinguished because it isn't fire running out of fuel — it's a hunger that doesn't know how to stop. **White Flame:** The inversion — pushing vitality in rather than pulling it out. Healing is the black flame run in reverse; he isn't learning something new, he's learning he was never only capable of destruction.
- **Backstory:** At age eight, his uncontrollable black flame killed his brother. His mother, unable to live with the grief, chose to wipe her own memories of her family and disappear into the layman world rather than stay. He's been alone with his father and a younger brother who was two at the time and remembers almost nothing. For seven years he hasn't used his power. During an earlier Trial-adjacent event, he ran and was chased down by the other rungs "like a dog" — traumatizing.
- **Want vs. need:** **Want** — to never hurt anyone again, which in practice means never using his power at all; he doesn't want to master it, he wants to be ordinary. **Need** — to forgive himself. Every loss in his life traces back to one moment he didn't understand what he was; his arc is about the eight-year-old boy who's been punishing himself ever since, not about the flame itself.
- **Book 1 arc:** Uses his power for the first time during the Trial — a crack in the wall, not the wall coming down. The fear doesn't leave, but he finds a kindred spirit in Thorne, also at the mercy of his own manifestation. When Thorne falls, Wyll takes up his thesis on manifestations and their control over a user's will — partly grief, partly self-preservation.
- **Connection to Thorne:** Thorne's thesis is the first time Wyll has seen his own experience reflected academically. Losing Thorne means losing the only framework he had for understanding himself.
- **Key relationships:** [[Alex Paige]], [[Trina]] — found family; [[Professor Thorne]] — kindred spirit, then loss.
- **Status tracker:** Alive, has used his power once (controlled crack, not full mastery), inherits Thorne's thesis.
- **Notes:**

### [[Tristan Orm]]

- **Role in story:** Rival and eventual love interest (not this book). The party's Tank and the bridge between Alex's world and the upper Rings. He is there to show Alex that not everyone from a "higher" class deserves her contempt — and she is there to show him the same in reverse.
- **Physical description:** _Not established._
- **Family:** The Orm name literally means dragon — a legacy of power, reputation, and expectation. His father is frequently abroad; his older brother [[Cayden Orm]] and two younger brothers all treat him as unworthy of the name. His mother is his only genuine relationship within the family, but she's occupied with internal affairs and largely unavailable.
- **The Orm legacy:** The family manifestation is draconic transformation — conjuring dragon parts and, at full awakening, full transformation. His grandfather achieved it; his father never did, and rather than absorb that failure himself, distributed the burden across his sons. Cayden took it all on himself, and it changed him. The brothers didn't fracture from hatred — they fractured because one of them tried to carry something alone that was never meant to be carried.
- **The core wound:** Tristan inherited his mother's manifestation instead of the family's dragon line. In a family where legacy is written in dragon fire, this marks him as a disappointment before he's done anything wrong.
- **Manifestation / Powers — The Shieldmaiden:** A pact with an otherworldly being. Conjures spectral shields with varying effects depending on the moment's need. Power is directly tied to devotion — the stronger his commitment to protecting someone, the stronger the shield. When his confidence falters or he has nothing he believes is worth protecting, the connection weakens. Right now his shields are weak not because he lacks ability, but because he's convinced himself he has nothing worth defending — his character growth and his magical growth are the exact same arc.
- **Want vs. need:** **Want** — to prove himself worthy of the Orm name, to earn respect from a family that's largely already decided he won't; he stakes everything on this constantly. **Need** — unconditional support. He's built his entire identity around a standard he'll never reach and needs to break that cycle before it finishes him — exactly what Alex's found family represents, and exactly why he's drawn into their orbit despite himself.
- **Act 3 beat:** [[Cayden Orm]]'s betrayal during the Trial isn't just a plot turn — it's Tristan's family doing what they always do, cutting him down in front of someone he was just beginning to trust. That it happens in front of Alex specifically is what makes it the wound it is.
- **Key relationships:** [[Cayden Orm]] — brother, living warning; [[Alex Paige]] — rivals turning into trust.
- **Arc:** Mutual dislike burns off across Acts 2–3 as Alex bests him twice, proving she isn't weak. Ends Book 1 in mutual trust, a nascent rivalry, and the beginning of something neither would name yet.
- **Status tracker:** Alive, injured and recovered after the Act 4 attack.
- **Notes:**

### Secondary Characters

#### [[Cayden Orm]] — First Ring, Tristan's Older Brother

- **Role:** The Act 3 betrayer and the living embodiment of what the Orm legacy does to people. He is not a rival — he is a warning. What Tristan could become if he keeps chasing the family standard instead of his own.
- **Manifestation / Powers:** Partial dragon transformation — claws, strength, fire. The most advanced of the Orm siblings. The power has almost certainly inserted its own influence over him rather than the other way around, much like Thorne.
- **State:** Beyond redemption within this book. Consumed by the family legacy and by his manifestation's influence to the point that power is the only metric he recognizes. Views his parents as placeholders, Tristan as an embarrassment, Alex and friends as beneath acknowledgment.
- **The Trial betrayal:** Fully calculated — he'd been tracking Tristan via a crystal the entire time, knew where he was and who he was with, and waited. Kills [[Heinz]] without hesitation, throws Alex into a wall without acknowledgment, leaves with a single command to Tristan: _"Come, little brother."_
- **Key line:** _"Did you think I would allow you to go off on your own? That I wouldn't notice you associating with the trash?"_ — He never needs to raise his voice.
- **Function in Tristan's arc:** Cayden has been the voice in Tristan's head his entire life. When he walks into the room, Tristan doesn't freeze out of cowardice — he freezes because Cayden has always been the proof that he doesn't measure up.
- **Status tracker:** Alive, at large at end of Book 1.

#### [[Amara Sol]] — Children of Cain, Chosen

- **Role:** The Children of Cain's cleaner — a background operative who eliminates loose ends. Assassin-adjacent. She's the one who silences [[Professor Thorne]] at the end of Act 4 and delivers the warning to Alex. In the wider trilogy she's the first real face of the Children and a long thread of potential redemption that may never fully resolve.
- **Manifestation / Powers:** Shadow manipulation — can transform and weaponize any shadow she can touch, for movement and combat. Cannot control the shadows of those more powerful than herself. Preferred weapon: a large scythe formed from shadow.
- **Backstory:** The Sol family was once prominent — Amara graduated from the Sanctum with honors. She joined the Children after losing her family, particularly her daughter; they weaponized her grief by framing her daughter's death as Truth's design — fated, inevitable, cosmically sanctioned. They broke her will with the one thing she couldn't argue against. She stole the **Book of Trials** (which dictates the trials within the system and how they're chosen each year) and passed it, along with his heritage, to [[Professor Thorne]] — the leverage that recruited him.[^9]
- **Within the Children:** Technically a Chosen — one of the Inheritor's small elite who carry the closest connection to the mark of Cain and operate as generals. Functions less as a general and more as a specialist who cleans up what others leave behind.
- **Trilogy function:** A warning sign and a loose thread — not a true believer, but a broken person who chose a story about fate because grief left her no other options. The door to redemption exists; she has done real damage and the story won't pretend otherwise.
- **Status tracker:** Active, at large at end of Book 1.

#### [[Cassian Vael]] — The Sanctum, Headmaster

- **Allegiance:** Good, though measured and sometimes calculated rather than warm.
- **Physical description:** Tall and lean; deep brown skin, warm undertone. Short black hair with the faintest threading of silver at the temples. Deep brown, almost black eyes — he "regards" rather than stares. Neat mustache and a long, thin, deliberately kept goatee. Impeccable dark navy pinstripe suits; a half-cape over one shoulder, fabric deep as the night sky with something that looks like drifting stars — enchanted or a trick of the material is a running student debate he never settles.
- **Personality:** Not warm exactly, but fair in a way that feels architectural — built in rather than chosen moment to moment. Rarely raises his voice; rarely needs to.
- **Book 1 role:** Background authority figure. Not a major player this book but carries significant weight in the larger story.
- **Key detail:** He was the one who assigned Alex's mother to the mission investigating Heaven — a secluded magical location the Children of Cain are using as a staging ground in partnership with RoxCorp. This decision is a ticking clock that will resurface.
- **Status:** His identity and physical description above come from a design canvas ([[Sanctum Faculty]]) rather than any prose note or the previous Story Bible — the old bible had this role listed as nameless with "gaps to fill." Worth confirming this is locked-in canon before it's used on the page. Manifestation still not established.[^11]

#### [[Aldric Burr]] — The Sanctum, Dean of Student Affairs

- **Role:** Not previously present in the Story Bible at all — found only in the [[Sanctum Faculty]] design canvas.[^12] The Sanctum's most vocal champion of the Ring system, not because anyone asked him to be but because he's decided, somewhere deep and unexamined, that the hierarchy is natural rather than arbitrary — which makes his favoritism toward First Ring students and open disdain for Third Ring students feel righteous in his own eyes.
- **Appointed:** 2009. **Age:** 52.
- **Physical description:** Short and round, moves with momentum rather than stillness. Pale, ruddy, perpetually flushed skin that betrays his emotions before he chooses to show them. Small, bright golden-brown eyes set a touch too close together, quick to narrow. Broad, slightly crooked nose (rumored broken by a student once). A dramatic, aggressively receding widow's peak he trims every two weeks in a losing battle to make it look intentional; remaining hair dark brown, oiled flat. Mustache and thin goatee clearly modeled on Headmaster [[Cassian Vael]]'s — on Vael it's precise, on Burr it reads as researched and doesn't quite land. Wears the faculty uniform a size too snug, favoring deep burgundy and dark olive to project authority; projects effort instead.
- **Personality:** Not evil — something more tiresome: a man of middling power who's mistaken his prejudices for principles. Loud in faculty meetings, overbearing in the halls, oddly quiet whenever Vael is in the room.
- **Key relationship:** Believes he and the Headmaster are close confidants; Vael treats him like furniture that occasionally needs polishing.
- **Status:** Same canon-confirmation flag as Vael above.[^11]

## 5. Setting & World

- **Time period / setting overview:** Contemporary Earth with a hidden magical world layered underneath it, concealed from non-magical people ("laymen") by [[The Veil]]. Story present-day year is not explicitly established.[^14]
- **Locations:**
    - **Name:** [[The Sanctum]]
        - **Description / atmosphere:** A magic school founded 1355, centered on Cornwall Hall (Gothic, original building) with Aegon Hall (recreation/training, home of the Cateferium dining hall), Helios Square (academic buildings around the carved Helios Forma sphere), the Garden Maze (hiding [[Caché Café]]), and three Ring-segregated dorms — Dominus (First Ring), Eskavar (Second Ring), Charter/"Brown Dorm" (Third Ring).
        - **Significance to plot:** Primary Book 1 setting; the Ring hierarchy (First "Blooms," Second, Third "Weeds") is the social engine of most of Alex's conflict.
        - **Sensory details:** See [[The Sanctum]] for full campus detail — living river motifs on Dominus Hall, weathered brown brick on Charter Hall, the Helios Forma's undecipherable carvings.
    - **Name:** [[Caché Café]]
        - **Description / atmosphere:** Hidden at the center of the Garden Maze, one of the only places on campus where Ring divisions are informally set aside. Run by Professor Albricht Emeer on a sliding-price scale.
        - **Significance to plot:** Social-neutral-ground location; worth keeping in mind for scenes where Alex needs space outside Ring politics.
        - **Sensory details:** Ash-and-caramel Frênedoré ("Fray") drinks, floral-and-coffee air, a matte-black modern kiosk against centuries-old hedges.
    - **Name:** Harmony Hills Academy
        - **Description / atmosphere:** A gray, prison-like non-magical boarding school in Rhode Island — Alex's placement immediately before the Sanctum. Depressing, institutional, "gray" in every sense (walls, food, staff).
        - **Significance to plot:** Establishes Alex's pre-magic world and her isolation; also where Mr. Terrell was already teaching, unbeknownst to her, as a history teacher.[^8]
    - **Name:** The Cabin
        - **Description / atmosphere:** Where Alex and her dad spent summers — hot chocolate, s'mores, a loft with a fire. Her strongest, and possibly only, sustained memory of closeness with him.
        - **Significance to plot:** Emotional anchor / "home" more than any actual residence is.
    - **Name:** [[Heaven]]
        - **Significance to plot:** Secluded magical location, Children of Cain staging ground in partnership with RoxCorp. Where Alex's mother was sent to investigate and subsequently captured.
- **Social structure:** The Sanctum's three-Ring hierarchy — First Ring (elite, most potential), Second Ring (nobility/prominent families not quite First Ring caliber), Third Ring (unaffiliated "commoners," heavily ridiculed). Membership is determined by whether a family's crest appears in the **Book of Families**. Unofficial slang: "Blooms" (First/Second Ring) vs. "Weeds" (Third Ring) — banned by the school, used constantly anyway.
- **Magic system / speculative rules:** Fully documented in [[Lore Bible]] — link straight to the relevant heading rather than duplicating here:
    - [[Lore Bible#The Weave]] — the substrate magic is drawn from
    - [[Lore Bible#The Veil]] — barrier concealing magic from non-magical people; hard rule, cannot be reapplied once broken (see Continuity Flags below)
    - [[Lore Bible#Manifestations]] — innate powers, their two-stage awakening, the open question of whether manifestations can influence their user (directly relevant to [[Professor Thorne]] and [[Cayden Orm]])
    - [[Lore Bible#Crests]] — spell encoding, the five-spell limit, the Fourth Year acquisition process
    - [[Lore Bible#The Cosmic Layer]] — Truth, Entropy, Keystones, the Children of Cain's cosmology
- **Culture & customs:** Sanctum students are still called "Magi" rather than "Operators," a holdover from the school's age. The Intra-School Tournament (Pillar, Dominion, Disc Gallery) runs every four years each February, timed to the "Magic Solstice" on the 20th.
- **Sensory & seasonal variation:** See individual location entries above; not yet developed for the wider setting beyond the Sanctum campus.

## 6. Timeline

- **Story present-day date/era:** Not explicitly dated. The setting reads as contemporary-Earth-adjacent (the Tournament's four-year cycle is explicitly tied to the real Gregorian leap year), but no in-story year has been fixed.[^14]

    1. ~8 years before present: Wyll's uncontrollable Black Flame kills his brother; his mother wipes her memories of her family and disappears.

    3. Before Book 1: Alex's father dies (a sacrifice); Alex awakens her own blessing in response.
    4. ~6 months before Chapter 1: Alex is placed at Harmony Hills Academy; Mr. Terrell is already there, teaching history.

    11. Act 5: Uneasy truce between Alex and Tristan; news surfaces about Alex's mother; Book 2 setup (Advancement Tournament) teased.

## 7. Plot Structure

- **Structure model used:** Five-Act.
- **Major beats:**
    - **Act 1:** After her father's death pulls her into a hidden world of magic, Alex begins training under Mr. Terrell and vows to find her missing mother at any cost.
    - **Act 2:** At the Sanctum, Alex is immediately targeted by those conspiring against her, finds an unlikely mentor in Thorne, earns her first spells, and forms uneasy bonds — including a sharp mutual dislike with the mysterious Tristan.
    - **Act 3 (Trial / midpoint):** During the school's Trial, Alex reluctantly teams with Tristan to hunt a "vampire," only to crack the case temporarily before being outmaneuvered by First Ring students and betrayed by Tristan's brother — shattering whatever trust had formed between them.
    - **Act 4 (low point):** When students go missing and Tristan turns up injured, Alex leads her friends to confront Thorne — only to watch the Children of Cain silence him before he can reveal what he knows, leaving her with a chilling warning.
    - **Act 5 (resolution):** Alex reaches an uneasy truce with Tristan, processes the year's losses, and vows to get stronger — she wasn't ready this time, and she knows it.
- **Subplots:**
    - The Trial mystery — Thorne's ancestor, the framing of Alex, resolves into Act 4's confrontation.
    - Wyll's manifestation — first use at the Trial, carries into Book 2 via Thorne's inherited thesis.
    - Trina's device — ongoing across the trilogy, partial progress only in Book 1.
    - Alex's search for her mother — dream clues across Acts 2–4, ongoing into Book 2.

## 8. Continuity & Fact Log

- **Established facts:**
    - **[[Alex Paige]]** — Hair silver/gray, cropped at the shoulders, messy, cannot be dyed. Eyes green.
    - **[[The Sanctum]]** — Symbol: a griffin. Colors: maroon and gold.[^16]
    - **Children of Cain** — Symbol: a crude eye struck through by a slash, copied from the [[Mark of Cain]] itself. Dress: deep violet cloaks with silver trim (not black/red).
    - **[[Lore Bible#The Veil|The Veil]]** — Placed over non-magical people to prevent perceiving magic. Most children have one from birth, removed around ages 6–8. Can be artificially applied to someone not born with one. Weakens over time until it breaks entirely. **Once broken, it cannot be reapplied to that person — hard rule, no exceptions.**
- **Open questions / unresolved threads** _(in the order they surface)_:
    1. What happened to Alex's mother and where is she?
    2. Who or what is responsible for the strange occurrences at the Sanctum?
    3. Why is Alex being targeted specifically?
    4. What is Thorne hiding, and why does he seem to know more than he should?
    5. What are the Children of Cain and what do they actually want?
    6. What is a Keystone and why does it matter?
    7. What is Truth and what does it want with Alex?
- **Known inconsistencies to fix:**
    - **Trial timing vs. grade level.** Quick Plot Notes state "trials start in year three" and that Alex's class has already been "pummeled" by other Rings for two prior years — implying Alex's cohort is now in at least their third occurrence of the Trial. But [[The Sanctum]]'s year/grade table ties Crest acquisition to Fourth Year, Alex is actively using a 3-spell Crest that was recently re-encoded, and her stated age (15) maps to Fourth Year on that same table. Worth pinning down which year Alex is actually in during Book 1, and whether "the Trial" (Act 3's event) is the same institution as Crest acquisition or a separate, earlier hazing ritual.
    - **Headmaster and Dean canon status.** [[Cassian Vael]] (Headmaster) and [[Aldric Burr]] (Dean) both come from a design canvas, not from any prose draft or the previous Story Bible — flagged above in Section 3, repeated here since it affects Sanctum-hierarchy continuity broadly.[^11]
