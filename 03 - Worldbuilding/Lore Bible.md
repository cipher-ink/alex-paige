## The Weave

The Weave is the physical embodiment of the **Concept of Connection** — an immaterial force that exists at all points in time and space simultaneously. It has no beginning and no end; to the Weave, there is no such thing as time. It simply _is_, and always has been.

The Weave interconnects all things in existence — living and non-living, material and immaterial. To those with the ability to perceive it clearly, it resembles a silken tapestry or a spider's web, with threads running between every person, object, and plane of reality.

> **The Weave is not magic.** Magic is the name given by those who learned to manipulate it. The Weave itself is simply the underlying fabric of reality.

### Manipulation

By pulling, redirecting, or altering the threads of the Weave, a practitioner can produce powerful real-world effects. The Weave functions as a source of raw energy — a catalyst for reaction. A practitioner might manipulate the Weave to ignite oxygen in the air and produce fire from nothing.

Beyond energy, the Weave has a second, more unsettling property: it can alter **Concepts** themselves. This is where magic transcends the merely physical. Altering physics, influencing minds, summoning extraplanar entities — these effects don't come from raw energy alone, but from the Weave's ability to rewrite the fundamental nature of things. In this sense, the Weave behaves somewhat like radiation — invisible, pervasive, and capable of fundamentally transforming whatever it touches.

### The Immaterial

_Immaterial_ refers broadly to anything that cannot be physically interacted with — typically inhabitants or phenomena of the Conceptual and Spiritual planes. The Weave connects material and immaterial alike, which is what makes cross-planar phenomena possible at all.

### Leylines

Leylines are connection points between two different planes of existence, formed naturally by the Weave. They function like tears in the fabric of reality — doorways between planes, whether material-to-material or material-to-immaterial.

Magic is significantly stronger in proximity to leylines. They are not static; they open and close in cycles tied to Earth's orbit around the sun. The leading theory is that gravitational effects on the Weave drive these cycles, though it remains unproven.

The **Magic Solstice** — occurring in February — marks the annual period when leylines between the material and immaterial planes are at their most permeable. During this window, casting magic becomes easier and more potent across the board, which is why the Intra-School Tournament is timed to coincide with it.

## The Practitioner

### Cultural Traditions of Magic

Magic is not practiced uniformly across the world. Different cultures developed their own relationships with the Weave independently, and those traditions persist today — sometimes in tension with one another.

**Western Tradition (European/American)** The dominant framework at institutions like the Sanctum. Magic here is treated as a science — something to be studied, categorized, and understood at a physical level. This tradition gave rise to standardized terminology: _mana_, _affinity_, _class_, _focus_. It prizes research, documentation, and reproducibility. The Sanctum represents the apex of this tradition: structured curriculum, ranked students, and magic as a discipline to be mastered through accumulated knowledge.

**Eastern Tradition** Rather than studying magic from the outside, Eastern practice is oriented inward. Practitioners — who call the energy _prana_ — seek a deeper spiritual connection with the Weave through the self. The casting style takes the form of a martial art: a traditional dance through which prana flows through the body in controlled movements, channeled rather than commanded. Power here is a byproduct of self-knowledge and enlightenment, not academic achievement. The two traditions arrive at similar results through fundamentally different philosophies.

**Religious Traditions** Magic has also taken root in various religious contexts across the world, where it is understood through spiritual or devotional frameworks. These traditions are numerous and varied and remain an area of ongoing discovery.

---

### Mana

The energy of the Weave that practitioners draw on is called many things depending on tradition. In the Western tradition it is called **mana**. In the Indian subcontinent it is known as **prana**. Other cultures have their own names, each carrying the assumptions and philosophy of the tradition that coined it. Regardless of what it's called, the substance is the same: the raw energy of the Weave, drawn through a practitioner's body and shaped into effect.

---

### Affinities

An affinity is one's natural aptitude toward a style of magic. People are born with an affinity — it is not chosen or developed, only discovered.

While mortals classify magic into four main schools and many subschools, an affinity can be almost anything. From broad elemental forces like fire, to something as specific as the positive charge of an electron. The more complicated the affinity, the harder it is to fully understand. Some practitioners live their entire lives only grasping the broad strokes of theirs.

Regardless of conscious understanding, a person will naturally gravitate toward the kind of magic that aligns with their affinity — it simply comes easier. Affinities are discovered in Second Year at the Sanctum through the Attunements course.

---

### The Focus

A focus is not optional — it is a fundamental requirement of casting. Mana must have something to flow _through_, be shaped by, and finally be _released_ from. Without a focus, there is nowhere for that energy to go.

The body itself acts as a **conduit** — mana flows through the circulatory system from external sources into the practitioner and toward the focus. The focus then compiles and releases that energy as the intended effect. The more control a practitioner has over the flow, the less they need an elaborate external focus — but something must always serve that final shaping and release function.

**Beryllium** is the premier focus material. A naturally mana-tuned mineral, it allows mana to pass through it cleanly and be shaped with precision. Early practitioners used large Beryllium crystals. In a pinch, a practitioner can use their own body as both conduit _and_ focus — but this is inherently dangerous, as the body was not designed to bear that final release.

---

### What Casting Feels Like

**Without a crest**, casting magic is a full-body experience. As mana channels through the circulatory system it produces a deep, spreading warmth — often described as drinking hot tea on a very cold day. The warmth builds as mana moves through the body and concentrates as it approaches the focus, then releases in a single directed burst.

The physical cost is real. Controlling mana flow requires active effort — not unlike trying to direct your own blood by consciously contracting and relaxing muscles. Extended or powerful casting is exhausting. Losing control is dangerous: uncontrolled mana can spiral and build within the body, burning and damaging from the inside out.

**With a crest**, the crest draws mana from the surrounding air rather than pulling it through the practitioner's body, and the encoded spell handles the shaping automatically. The caster feels primarily a warmth concentrated on their arm as mana gathers at the crest. It is significantly less taxing — which is precisely why the crest changed everything.

### The Evolution of Casting — A Brief History

The history of magical practice is essentially the history of the focus.

1. **Early manipulation** — The first practitioners used only their own bodies as conduits, with no external focus. Powerful, dangerous, and limited.
2. **Discovery of Beryllium** — The identification of Beryllium as a naturally mana-tuned mineral gave practitioners a reliable external focus, dramatically improving safety and precision.
3. **Blood as catalyst** — The discovery that life force — most accessibly through blood — could act as a natural catalyst for mana gave rise to blood magic and ritualistic practice.
4. **Combined practice** — Combining blood as catalyst with a Beryllium focus amplified a practitioner's power significantly.
5. **The proto-crest** — Powderized Beryllium embedded directly into the skin maintained permanent contact with the blood without requiring the practitioner to draw it manually.
6. **Runic language** — The development of enchantments and runic languages introduced a way to inscribe intent directly onto objects and materials.
7. **Spells encoded onto crests** — Combining runic with the proto-crest allowed spells to be embedded directly into the crest, enabling near-instant casting.
8. **The modern crest** — As the runic language grew more sophisticated, more spells could be encoded onto a single crest. The current standard is five embedded spells.

### The Psychological Shift

The technological evolution had a cultural consequence. As casting became faster, easier, and more accessible, magic stopped feeling like something earned and started feeling like something ambient. Most people in the modern era take magic for granted the way contemporary teenagers take the internet for granted. Counter-measures, regulations, and protections had to be developed to manage a world where power was suddenly easy to access. The tension between modern convenience and older traditions — Eastern, ritualistic, or otherwise — sits quietly beneath the surface of institutions like the Sanctum.

## Manifestations

A manifestation is exactly what the name implies: a physical expression of a person's **Concept** — their fundamental self made into power. While manifestations draw on the Weave as an energy source, they are intrinsically tied to the individual and thus are never truly identical between two people.

Some people never awaken a manifestation at all.

### Inheritance & Blood Marks

Manifestations can be passed down through **Blood Marks** — the magical inheritance encoded in bloodlines. However, an inherited manifestation does not transfer unchanged. It adapts to align with its new user, sometimes subtly and sometimes dramatically.

> **Example:** Alex's mother can generate light by exciting the atoms and photons around her — she is a source. Alex can manipulate light, but cannot create it. She alters the density and weight of photons, requiring them to already be present. Same fundamental power. Entirely different expression.

### The Two Stages

**Convergence** The first stage — the moment a manifestation initially appears. This typically happens naturally as a person ages, though it can be blocked. When the pathway is obstructed, it must be forcibly opened.

At convergence, a manifestation is unrefined. Two people who have both converged a fire-based manifestation may appear to have identical powers at this stage — because the manifestation has not yet fully differentiated itself. It exists, but it has not yet become _theirs_.

**Awakening** The second stage, and entirely personal. This is where the manifestation takes its true form — adapting completely to the user and expressing itself in the shape that most reflects who they are and what they want most deeply.

A fire user at convergence controls flame. The same person at awakening might manifest blazing wings, because flight — freedom, escape, transcendence — is what that power means _to them_.

Awakening cannot be rushed by study or technique alone. It comes through **training, hardship, and psychological growth**. A manifestation awakens when the person does.

## Crests

A crest is a magical tattoo — the modern evolution of the proto-crest — made from **Beryllium powder** mixed with traditional tattoo ink in a glycerin base. The Beryllium content means it cannot be applied with modern tattooing equipment; only a traditional hand-powered pin and needle instrument can be used.

Getting a crest feels much like getting a conventional tattoo, with one addition: as the Beryllium first makes contact with the recipient's blood, it produces a distinct warmth and small magical sparks as the material attunes to the user. The process typically takes one to two hours regardless of complexity.

Specialized practitioners called **Encoders** design and apply crests. The role blends tattooist and programmer — encoding requires significant training, as an improperly constructed crest can backfire dangerously. Encoding schools exist across the country.

>[!NOTE]
>Most people cannot read a crest in any technical sense -- they cannot identify spells, parse the runic, or determine what a crest does by looking at it. What they CAN do is read it in the way one might read a tattoo: placement, scale, density, visual weight, and era. A practitioner with experience can roughly date both the base design and encoded layer on sight, and read the gap between them as a rough measure of one's bloodline history.
>This can also be taken advantage of by some designers who prefer to use older designs to appear "older" or more intimidating at a glance to those who know what to look for.
#### Crest Designs
How a crest looks is determined by two things: **who** encoded it and **when**. For first-generation crest recipients, and **Encoder** designs the Path from scratch -- a process closer to programming than portraiture. The encoder makes technical decisions on how to construct each spell, and the visual result is a byproduct of that construction. Encoders can take input from the recipient and make have strong personal aesthetics, but the Path goes where the Runic requires it to go. Two encoders building the same five spells will produce two crests that have similarities, but look almost nothing alike.

For those carrying a **Blood Mark**, the process splits into two distinct layers. The base design -- the ancestral Path tied to that bloodline -- is fixed. The encoder's work is overridden and the crest resolves into the inherited design, unchanged from the first time it was ever applied. No encoder can alter this part. But the encoded spells sitting on top of that base are a separate matter. Spells can be added, removed, and replaced using whatever forme of runic is current, and those additions are visually distinct from the base. the base design reflects the era of the ancestor who first bore it. The encoded spells reflect the era in which they were added. On a crest with a long history, these two layers may look nothing alike -- though for the most part encoders will try to maintain the artistic "style" of the base.

To anyone who knows what they are looking at, this is immediatly readable. A Proto-era base with modern spell encoding looks like two different hands worked on it, because they did. The older the bloodline, the more visible that gap can become.

This means that inherited crests are effectively historical documents. How old a bloodline is, and how actively it's crest is maintained and re-encoded over time, can often be read at a glance.
###### The Proto-Crest Era
> *(Roughly 900 A.D. and before)*

The oldest surviving crest designs predate the standardized formes of runic entirely. Encoded using sparse and heavy lines, thick and unbroken strokes, long straight lines, and minimal ornamentation. Not primitive in intent, but in capacity since runic had not evolved to store spells yet. These crests look closer to brands or carved stonework rather than tattoos. A family carrying a proto-era base design is carrying something so old it predates most of the institutions that currently study magic.
###### The Classical Era
> *(~900 to 1300 A.D)*

As 2nd forme runic matured, encoders gained enough flexibility to make deliberate choices about structure. The Classical era is defined by the emergence of intentional design -- symmetry, geometric repetition, and the first regional differences in style. Schools of encoding in different parts of the world began developing distinct visual vocabularies: some favoring strict angular geometry, others favoring elongated organic curves. Classical crests are still relatively sparse, but they feel deliberate in a way that proto-crest era designs don't.
###### The Renaissance
> *(~1300 to 1800 A.D)*

By the time 3rd runic forme reached it's maturity, encoders had more expressive latitude in Path construction that they had ever had before -- and it shows. This era is defined by excess: intricate interlocking linework, fine detail that borders on illegible up close, ornamentation layered over ornamentation. Old noble families from this period wear crests that look overwhelming at a first glance and reveal more the longer you look. The era reflects a period when displaying the complexity of one's crest was itself a status symbol -- the more elaborate the work, the more powerful and prestigious the bloodline.
###### The Reformation Era
> *(1800 A.D to present)*

As a deliberate reaction to the Renaissance, the reformation era stripped back -- cleaner lines, intentional negative space, precision in service of readability. The craft is no less sophisticated, but the philosophy reversed: confidence expressed through restraint rather than ornamentation. Reformation crests tend to feel "modern" or "neo" in style. This is the dominant aesthetic of newly encoded crests today, though some choose to continue the "older" styles or choose their own paths as more and more encoders join the workforce. 
### Spell Anatomy

Every spell encoded onto a crest has three components:

**The Root** The base of the spell. Roots are standardized by school of magic — all evocation spells share the same root, all conjuration spells share theirs. On the crest itself the root appears as a solid black band, though it is not truly solid. It is hundreds of runic symbols woven so tightly together they become visually indistinguishable from a single line.

**The Path** The actual encoding of the spell — what it does, its effects, its cost, its behavior. This is the artistic portion of the crest, unique to each user. The twisting, turning linework that makes every crest visually distinct is the Path.

**The Wall** A thin black line at the terminus of the spell. The Wall acts as a cap — a guardrail that limits the spell to a defined power ceiling. Without it, excess mana could overload the spell and cause it to malfunction or blow back on the caster.

### Crest Structure

Visually, a crest reads from one end to the other: a thick black band (the Root), an intricate winding design (the Path), and a thin black line (the Wall). What looks like artwork is in fact a densely layered magical document.

### The Five Spell Limit

Each spell encoded onto a crest requires a permanent, active connection to the user's blood — and through it, their Concept. This connection is always on; it cannot be switched off without removing the encoding entirely. This is what makes crest spells feel instinctive — the spell is already hotwired into the user's biology.

That constant connection carries a physical and metaphysical toll on both the user and their Concept. Five simultaneous connections is the current limit — a reflection of where runic language currently stands, not an arbitrary convention. The limit began at one spell and has grown as runic evolved. Whether it can be pushed beyond five is an open question.

A crest with no spells encoded still functions as a focus. Blank crests are rare in the modern era but not unheard of.

### Swapping Spells

Spells can be replaced, but not quickly. An Encoder must carefully untangle and remove the existing Path before a new one can be laid in its place. Depending on how many spells need replacing, this can take three to four hours. A crest should never be left mid-process — an unfinished or misaligned crest has a significant chance of backfiring. Encoders always ask about Blood Marks before beginning work, since a Blood Mark will override their design entirely.

### Blood Marks

Blood Marks are genetic markers passed down through family lines. They are the mechanism by which affinities, manifestations, and crest designs are inherited.

When a crest is applied to someone carrying a Blood Mark, the encoder's design is overridden — the crest shifts to reflect the ancestral design tied to that bloodline.

More significantly, Blood Marks carry an **ancestral knowledge pool**. The crest does not transmit spells directly, but it passes down something like deeply embedded memory — closer to magical muscle memory than conscious knowledge. If an ancestor spent forty years mastering a particular spell, their descendant may find that same spell comes naturally in twenty. Practitioners describe it not as inherited information but as inherited _familiarity_ — as though the Weave itself remembers on their behalf.

## Spell Structure

### Casting Methods

Spells are, at their core, a finessing of the Weave — and there are as many ways to do that as there are cultures that practice magic. A practitioner might use a dance-like martial form, recite a long incantation, make a ritualistic sacrifice on an inscribed altar, or use purely mental visualization.

As magical study advanced, the **verbal component** emerged as the most universally effective tool — not because words have inherent power, but because speaking aloud is one of the most reliable ways to anchor visualization. A practitioner might say _"flame"_ while conjuring fire, or _"wind"_ while creating a cutting gust. The word is a focusing mechanism, not an incantation.

For **encoded crest spells**, the verbal component becomes simply a trigger word. The spell's construction is already handled by the encoding.

For **non-encoded spells**, the caster must understand and consciously manage every component in the moment: how mana needs to flow, how it must be altered or attuned, whether a material component is required, and what parameters need to be set — range, duration, intensity, and so on.

### Spell Mathematics

Spell Mathematics is the formal study of **spell parameters** — the variables a caster manipulates to shape what a spell actually does in practice.

Simple spells are easy. Conjuring a ball of fire requires little parameter work. But creating a whip _made_ of fire — controlling its length, flexibility, heat distribution, and duration simultaneously — requires a thorough understanding of how each variable interacts.

Parameters can be encoded directly into a crest spell, locking them in permanently. This makes complex spells more reliable but also more rigid. A simple spell with no encoded parameters can be adapted to almost any situation by a skilled caster.

This is the central trade-off of crest design: with only five spell slots, a practitioner must choose spells flexible enough to be useful across a wide range of scenarios. Highly specialized spells burn a slot on a single use case.

### Spell Authorship

Spells are a studied and documented tradition — grimoires and academic texts record spells and their construction in detail. But this does not mean all spells that achieve the same effect are identical.

Two practitioners can independently develop spells that produce the same result through entirely different construction. The Root and Wall may be similar, but the Path can differ completely. One fire spell might continuously draw on ambient oxygen as fuel. Another might pull energy from a separate plane of existence through conjuration. Same flame. Entirely different architecture.

### The Four Schools of Magic

All magic can be categorized into one of four schools. These schools are intentionally broad — they describe the _nature_ of what a spell does, not how it does it. Each school contains dozens of **subschools** that further categorize spells by method, target, or application. There are currently hundreds of recognized subschools.

**Evocation** Magic that draws energy from the Weave or the environment and releases it as a direct effect. Most elemental magic falls here — fire, lightning, force. Evocation is about output.

**Conjuration** Magic that calls something into being or establishes a connection with another plane of existence. Summoning entities, opening planar channels, and necromancy — raising or communicating with the dead — are all conjuration. So is contract magic, in which the caster forms a binding agreement with an otherworldly being.

**Illusion** Magic that alters perception — of the caster, of a target, or of reality itself. Illusion operates on the mind and the senses rather than the physical world directly.

**Enchantment** Magic that alters the properties of an object or being by rewriting their Concept. Runics, magical technology, and imbuing objects with persistent effects are all enchantment. **Living Transmutation** — applying enchantment magic to a living being to alter them at a fundamental level — falls under this school and is gravely frowned upon in the modern era.

## Sub-disciplines

### Contracts

Before the sub-disciplines can be understood individually, they share a common philosophical root: the **Contract**.

A Contract is a formal agreement with the metaphysical embodiment of the Weave itself. The underlying law is **equivalent exchange** — you cannot gain something without giving something of proportional value in return. Contracts are not a loophole in magic's rules. They _are_ one of its fundamental rules. Ritual Magic and Alchemy are both expressions of this principle, approached from different directions.

---

### Ritual Magic

Ritual Magic is Contract in its purest form. A practitioner agrees to sacrifice something — money, time, life force, a material object, or something more abstract — in exchange for a specific outcome. The two things exchanged do not need to be related. You might sacrifice years of your life to locate a lost person, or destroy something of great personal value to gain power. What matters is that the cost is proportional to what is asked.

There is no standardized ritual. Many have been documented over centuries and are considered reliable, but rituals are as varied as the people who create them and the outcomes they seek.

---

### Alchemy

Where Ritual Magic exchanges two unrelated things, Alchemy concerns itself with **transformation** — changing one thing directly into another. It is Contract applied to connection itself.

The law of equivalent exchange governs Alchemy strictly. You cannot transmute something into a form that exceeds the value of what you started with. If the desired result requires more than you have, you must provide the additional material or energy yourself.

Alchemy includes potion-making in the traditional sense, but extends far beyond that into the transmutation of materials, states, and properties. It is one of the oldest disciplines precisely because it deals with the most fundamental act the Weave enables: changing the nature of what something _is_.

---

### Runics

Runics is the written language of magic — developed specifically for the encoding and inscription of spells onto objects and crests. It is not a spoken language and has no conversational use. Its entire purpose is the consumption and direction of magical effect.

There are currently four Formes of Runic:

|Forme|Status|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|1st Forme|Obsolete|The archaic original. Studied academically, like Latin to modern English.|
|2nd Forme|Largely obsolete|Still practiced by a small number of practitioners. Used to inscribe the earliest crests and enchanted objects.|
|3rd Forme|Current standard|As compact and information-dense as has been considered mortally achievable. The vast majority of modern crests and enchantments use this.|
|4th Forme|Specialized|Developed exclusively for magical technology. A translation layer between the Weave and inorganic technology. Incompatible with organic matter.|

> **Note on 4th Forme:** It was designed for inorganic matter only. Organic material is too dynamic and ambiguous for 4th Forme to handle — it requires the finesse and flexibility that a statically structured language cannot provide. The analogy in programming terms: the difference between a statically typed and a dynamically typed language.

---

### Magical Technology

Magical technology — **magitech** — is the product of 4th Forme Runic applied to the physical world. By allowing technology to interface directly with the Weave, practitioners have created devices that draw on magical energy rather than conventional power sources.

Practical examples include phones powered by the Weave, lights that never burn out, and lighters that never run out of fuel. The field is young relative to other disciplines and is considered one of the most rapidly evolving areas of magical study.

---

### Conjuration

Conjuration is the act of projecting one's Concept outward to reach another plane of existence and make contact with whatever resides there. The precise mechanism is not fully understood — how the Concept crosses planar boundaries, and where exactly these other planes exist in relation to the material world, remains one of magic's open questions.

These other planes operate under entirely different rules than the material world. The beings that inhabit them are conscious, autonomous entities with their own agendas — not tools. Conjuration is therefore either an act of **force** — taking something from another plane against its will — or an act of **Contract** — forming an agreement with a being from that plane.

**Golems** The one exception to naturally occurring Concepts is the **Golem** — an artificially created being given an empty Concept by its maker. Because empty Concepts do not occur naturally, creating one requires deliberately hollowing out or fabricating the metaphysical foundation of a living thing. This practice is considered one of the great taboos of the magical world, sitting at the intersection of Living Transmutation and Conjuration.

## The Cosmic Layer

### The Nothing

Beyond the Weave — beyond time, space, and even Concept — exists the **Nothing**. It is not darkness, not emptiness in any conventional sense. It is the literal embodiment of the absence of everything. No time passes there. No space exists. Not even a Concept can take root. It is, by definition, beyond comprehension.

To those who have glimpsed it, it appears as a pure vantablack void — a spot in space that does not merely look dark, but looks like the _absence_ of the ability to look. The only things that exist within the Nothing are Truth and Entropy.

---

### Truth and Entropy

Truth and Entropy are entities that exist outside of and prior to everything — including the Weave, which has no power over them. They did not create the Weave. They simply watched as it came into being.

Because they exist in a state that mortal minds cannot process, humanity has always humanized them — given them shape, personality, and motive — as the only available framework for understanding them.

**Truth** embodies knowledge and balance. Truth sees all things — every possible future, every path a decision could take. This is not omniscience in the conventional sense. Truth does not know what a person will _choose_ before they choose it. Rather, Truth has seen every possible choice and its consequences simultaneously. The future is not fixed. It is a vast web of possibility, and which thread gets pulled is determined only in the moment of action.

**Entropy** embodies chaos and change. Not malevolence — Entropy does not seek destruction for its own sake. Its chaos operates on an individualistic level: the natural tendency of all things to follow their own course without order, structure, or community.

Neither Truth nor Entropy is good. Neither is evil. They do not oppose one another. They exist in perpetual balance — and the world functions because that balance holds. Their influence on the material world is passive. Their presence is simply _felt_ because they embody themselves, and what they embody permeates everything.

---

### Truth's Blessing

Witnessing Truth or Entropy directly requires two things: a profound depth of emotion and an already extraordinary connection to the Weave. It is an event so rare it may go hundreds of generations without occurring in any given bloodline.

The experience itself defies description. What is known is the aftermath: those who witness Truth and survive are granted the **Blessing** — sacred knowledge placed directly into the mind all at once. The human mind is not built to receive it. The process kills nearly everyone it touches. Those who survive carry something fundamentally different inside them from that point forward.

Once granted, the Blessing passes down a bloodline permanently.

---

### Keystones

**Keystone** is the name the Children of Cain use for those who carry Truth's Blessing. The name reflects their belief that these individuals are the literal key to Truth's realm — the only ones whose connection to Truth is strong enough to open a pathway to the Nothing.

It is both a title and a target.

---

### The Children of Cain

The Children of Cain are built around a philosophical conviction: that the existence of Truth constitutes a denial of free will. If Truth sees all and knows all, they reason, then choice is an illusion.

This reading is **incorrect**. Truth sees every possible future simultaneously — but does not know which one will be chosen until it happens. The future is not predetermined. Free will is real. The Children have simply misunderstood the nature of what they are fighting against.

Their goal is to use the Keystones to open a pathway to the Nothing and allow Entropy greater influence over the material world. They believe this will sever humanity from Truth's perceived control and grant true free will. What they would actually produce is a world in which order, community, and shared meaning dissolve entirely. Not liberation. Atomization.

The Children of Cain are not an ancient cult with deep roots and long tradition. They are a recent organization, built by someone who found the Mark of Cain and recognized its potential not as a philosophical mission but as a brand. The ancient-sounding name is a costume. Underneath it is something far more modern and recognizable: a cult of personality.

The current holder of the Mark — and the organization's leader — is **Richter Stalworth**.

---

### Cain & The Mark of Cain

Cain is both the biblical first murderer and a genuine historical figure in this world — the two are one and the same, recontextualized through Contract magic.

Cain attempted to make a Contract with the Weave and failed to fulfill his end of the bargain. The punishment for breaking a Contract at that fundamental level was the **Mark of Cain** — a curse, not a gift. So long as Cain bore the Mark, he could not die. He aged naturally but could not be killed by any hand, including his own.

The Mark can be transferred through a new Contract — passed from one host to another — at which point the previous bearer is finally permitted to die. **Cain himself is long dead.** The Mark has traveled through history ever since, moving from host to host across centuries. Wars, famines, and catastrophes trail behind the Mark's lineage like a wake. Whether those disasters were caused deliberately or are simply the natural consequence of placing unchecked immortality into flawed human hands is a matter of historical debate.

The original meaning and purpose of the Mark — if it ever had one beyond punishment — has been entirely lost to time.

**The subordinates** of the Mark's bearer don't carry any physical mark themselves. Instead they receive a lesser blessing through their association with the holder — unnatural healing, recovery beyond normal human limits, but nothing approaching true immortality. Only the bearer of the Mark cannot die.

---

### Richter Stalworth

The current holder of the Mark of Cain and founder of the Children of Cain. Richter found the Mark and recognized its potential — not as a philosophical calling, but as a tool for reputation and power. The ideology surrounding Truth, Entropy, and free will may be genuine to some members of his organization, but at its origin it was constructed to serve one person's ambitions.
