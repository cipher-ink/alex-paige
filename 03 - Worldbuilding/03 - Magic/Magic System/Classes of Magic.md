# Classes of Magic
Throughout years of study, it was discovered that spells crafted from [[The Weave]] tend to fall into certain categories based on how they are structured. The [[Spell Layers#The Core|core]] of a spell is always crafted in one of four different ways, thus giving rise to the four schools of magic.
### School vs Class
The terms school and class are used interchangeably when referring to the different cores that make up a spell, all depending on who you are talking to and how they were educated. Most [[Encoders|Encoders]] will use the term ‘Class’ as that was the original term, however in most educational instances you will hear the term ‘School’.
## Evocation
Evocation spells have to do with the creation of something tangible and physical. A ball of fire, light, or a sound can all be classified as evocation. When a user casts an Evocation spell, the [[Spell Layers#The Core|spell core]] is converting essence from the Weave, gathering the energy necessary from the Weave and the user’s own [[Mana 1|mana]].
## Illusion
Illusion spells are perception-based and have to do with the creation of things that aren’t really there. The core of this spell primarily takes it’s energy from the user’s mana rather than from the Weave. The more intense the Illusion, the more required from the caster. Mirages, dream-walking, and camouflage are good examples. Because Illusion spells are based on perception, they are the most finicky class of magic due to being broken easily once their “tricks” are known. 

Depending on the difference in will between the user and the target, tangible things can come from illusions. Giving someone a heart attack from fear, or even conjuring a fire so real that it actually burns.
## Conjuration
Conjuration spells have an innate connection to the [[Spiritual Plane|spiritual plane]] since it involves the connection of two [[Concepts|concepts]]. The core of the spell uses the energy of the Weave to reach beyond the [[Physical Plane|physical plane]] and create a bond between the spirit of the caster and that of their target. Summoning a familiar, a sentient weapon, teleportation, and telepathy are good examples of conjuration.

Because Conjuration spells are making a direct connection between two or more Concepts, the will of that target plays a large factor in the connection made. When trying to probe the mind of a person, if they are strong-willed enough they may be able to ward off the attack.
#### Contracts With Otherworldly entities
Due to the nature of Conjuration, it’s possible to create a contract between you and another entity for many different things. You could make a contract with a sentient weapon and summon it at will. You could be granted powers by a powerful being so long as you follow their rules.
#### Necromancy
Necromancy involves forging a connection with a being’s Concept even after they have passed on. There must still be an existing connection to the [[Physical Plane|physical plane]] in order to forge the contract, however that doesn’t have to be willingly.

While Concepts last forever, their connection to the physical world decays over time after they disappear, thus making it harder to forge a connection.
## Enchantment
The most difficult and hard to control class of magic. When casting an Enchantment Spell, rather than gathering essence from the Weave, the core of the spell is transmuting the already existing matter into something different. Transmuting gold, enhancing an objects hardness, and creating extra muscle are all examples of enchantment. Turning something into something else.

Due to [[Uler’s Law]], or the law of Equivalent Exchange, it’s required for those who use Enchantment magic to be very familiar with the structure of the things that they are trying to manipulate.
#### Enchanting Living Things
While possible, the enchanting of living things is generally frowned upon due to the messy consequences of failure. Should an enchantment fail, it backfires and all of the matter becomes stuck in a state of suspension between where it was originally and the intended destination. As the matter attempts to stabilize in any way it can, there is no sure way of knowing what will happen.
