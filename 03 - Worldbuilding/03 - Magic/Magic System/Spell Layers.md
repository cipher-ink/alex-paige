# Spell Layers
As the creation of spells became more commonplace through the ages, [[Encoders]] found that spells could be crafted through intricate circles and runes that would help them connect the [[Physical Plane|physical]] and [[Spiritual Plane|spiritual]] planes. Modern-day circuitry and electronics are based on the lines and connections of these spell circles.

As spells became more advanced and the need for easier ways to cast them grew, Encoders learned to generalize the creation of spell circles into three distinct layers.
### The Core
The center part of any spell circle, called the Core, is the heart of the spell. It is what determines how the spell functions and what [[Classes of Magic|class]] of magic it belongs to and the [[Affinities|affinity]] that it has.

Over time, the core became smaller and smaller as Encoders learned how to improve upon and standardize it’s creation.
### The Path
The largest part of the spell circle. The Path is responsible for the actual functioning of the spell. It determines the effects and how the energy gathered is put to use. The difference between creating a ball of fire and a bolt of lighting happens within this part of the spell.

There were originally only two layers to spells, however as more complex spells were crafted and more ways of calculating and [[Spell Inversion|inverting]] them were learned, the third layer needed to be added to act as a form of control and protection.
### The Wall
This part of the spell circle is relatively small and acts as a barrier for the spell, protecting it’s effects from going out of control as well as acting like an anti-inversion barrier, protecting the spell from being countered.
