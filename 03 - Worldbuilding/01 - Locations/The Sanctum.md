##### Established: 1355

The sanctum is a school made for teaching magic and housing those who show great potential for the future of Magi.

# General Layout
Assuming you are looking from the North.
## Center
**Cornwall Hall** dominates the middle ground - the original building at the founding of the Sanctum in 1355. Multiple stories, gothic architecture, and a long central tower. The Great Hall, Library, administrative offices, and traditional classrooms can be found here. This is also the location where the Book of Families is proudly displayed in the front foyer when not being used.
## East Side
- **Helios Square**: Curved academic buildings that wrap around a massive open courtyard with a massive 30-foot metal sphere floating in the center known as the **Helios Forma** which is covered with ancient carvings in a language no one can decipher. Students gather here between classes, sit on the curved steppes, and tell fortunes or spill rumors and traditions around the sphere.
- The Garden Maze: A large and winding hedge-maze with a small opening at the center where the [[Caché Café]] (literally means hidden cafe) opens its doors six days a week. It’s a student run cafe sponsored by [[Albricht Emeer]], the current Magic Ethics professor.
## West Side
- **Dominus Hall:** The smallest and most luxurious dorm, housing all of the first-ring students who stay for the semester. It’s plastered with white marble and teal/green terracotta patterns that shimmer and move as the river flowing through the entire campus seems to flow onto and around the walls of the dorm; the fish swim along it’s path with no issue. Not named after a person, but after the Greek word for “ruler”, signifying their positions above the other students.
- **Eskavar Hall:** The largest of the dorms, and the most modern by far as it tends to house the most students each semester and is thus given special treatment. It houses all of the second-ring students which are required to stay in the dorms for the semester since staying at home is a luxury for only the first-ring students. Named after [[Maria Eskavar]]: a [[Najiir]] alumni who participated in the creation of the third and fourth-spell crests.
- **Charter Hall**: The oldest and most neglected of the three dormitories, housing all third-ring students. Students decisively call it the “Brown Dorm” - the brown brick has weathered poorly over centuries, with visible water staining, crumbling mortar, and sections that are mismatched after being patched through eras of wear and tear. The windows are small and irregularly placed, with most having frames that are warped and don’t quite fit the window right. There are no special enchantments on the exterior like the other dorms - just functional, aging architecture. The name “charter" is said to symbolize “merit over means” for those of the third-rung attendees, though whether this is true is debatable.
# Social Hierarchy
The school is segregated into three kinds of students, called Rings. The Frist Ring are those at the top of their class. They show the most potential and are the elite. The Second Ring are nobility and prominent families who are not fit for the First Ring. The Third Ring is for those who are not associated with a family. Typically seen as "commoners", they are heavily ridiculed by the students and referred to using slang terms for the most part.

### The Book of Families
There is a book that contains the crest designs for all of the prominent families that the school has ever come in contact with. If you're crest is in the book, you are considered "royal" and are placed in either first or second ring. Otherwise, you are a commoner.

# Slang
Due to the age of the school, they still call magic users Magi instead of Operators.

There are unofficial terms used to represent the First, Second, and Third Rings. These terms are banned by the school, but that doesn't stop students from using them when the faculty are not around.

"Blooms" refers to those in the First and Second Ring. They are seen as the pinnacle of society and worthy of attending the school. It denotes beauty, cultivation, and a "natural selection" of sorts where only those deemed "most fit" will survive.

"Weeds" is the term used for those in the Third String. While any and all who attend the Sanctum show promise, the Third String is for those who are not associated with a particular family. The term denotes that they are unwanted, the trash of society, and not worthy of even attending the school in the first place, at least in the eyes of many of the families in the First and Second rings.

# Curriculum
The curriculum of the school is based around a quote of it's first headmaster, [[Alaeric Cornwall]]
> "To know Truth, fundamentally, is to know all."

While this phrase has been taken out of context, most within the school see it to mean that a foundational understanding of magic and how it works is key to being able to wield it.

### General Education
While this focuses on the magical education of the students, it's to be noted that all students are taking more generalized educational courses to keep in line with normal education standards. Reading, writing, math, history, and even science. Though most of these classes are given a magical "spin", meaning that most of what they learn will pertain to magic.

### First Years
First years at the Sanctum are taught mostly theory instead of practical application as they are deemed too young and immature to handle magic on their own. Most First Years haven't even awakened their manifestations.
	Classes
		Magic History, Basic Magical Theory, Creatures and Beings, Botany, Astronomy, and Mathematics

### Second Years
Second Years are finally able to start wielding practical magic in the form of learning how to manipulate the weave to create small effects within a controlled environment. They acquire a "focus" as a part of their learning materials to help them channel magic in these early days. This is where a student will discover their affinity and where they are given a Grimoire.
	Classes
		Ancient Practicum (spellcasting class), Alchemy, Attunements, Ecology, Enchantment Theory (Magic Tech), Grimoires

## Third Years
Now that students have started to practice magic, they are given more advanced classes and they start to learn more about their own powers such as manifestations and also delve into the more complex topic of ritual magic and basic crest theory before they get their crest as a Fourth Year. This is also the first year that students can join a team and participate in the Intra-School Competition where magic schools across the world compete in friendly games to be ranked among the top schools in the country.
	Classes
		Ritual Magic, Runics, Archeology, Basic Defensive Magics, Conjuration, Manifestations, Crest Theory

### Fourth Years
This is the year that students will acquire their crest and learn the more modern spellcasting lessons as well as the basics for dueling and offensive magic.
	Classes
		Spell Structure, Advanced Magic Theory, Spell Mathematics, Dueling, Special Electives (Based on students wants), Cosmology

### Fifth Years
Starting in Fifth Year, students are able to compete in the Advancement Tournament, which is held in the fall. Students within this year are supposed to form groups of three to four and register with the school for future years. These groups are essential for later years.
	Classes
		Magic Ethics and Law, Advanced Practicum, Enchantment Practicum (Creating magic tech), Contracts.

### Sixth Years
This is the year that students are able to be assigned quests, letting them venture into the world. Quests can be anything from a fetch quest, an internship, or a study abroad program. These are almost seen as independent studies and are always done with their assigned groups. From this point on, the students have fewer classes, but more responsibilities.
	Classes
		Quests (Independent Study), Magic Culture, Group Dynamics (Leadership classes)

### Seventh Years
In their final year at the Sanctum, they only really have two classes. Advanced Dueling and Layman Studies. The rest of the year and the students time is taken up by their thesis project. Here, they are supposed to work with their group to create a presentation on something related to the magical world. Some presentations have gone on to revolutionize the world of magic. 
	Classes
		Thesis, Advanced Dueling, Layman Studies

# Intra-School Tournament
This tournament is held every 4 years between 6 of the top magic schools in the world. The tournament itself is held in the spring during February and takes place over the course of 9 days starting on the 20th, each with a different event. The 20th was chosen due to it being the "Magic Solstice", which denotes the time of year when the leylines separating the material and immaterial weaken. This makes casting magic easier and more potent for the next month or so. This makes it so that the competition is always at it's most fierce and truly gives the students a time to shine.

This is why the Gregorian calendar has a leap year every 4 years, because the schools didn't want the tournament to extend into the next month for curriculum reasons.

The tournament is mostly for fun but also helps to determine the order to which the schools are ranked. It is a very competitive environment with the pride of most of the school at stake.

### Tournament Events
#### Pillar
This game is a test of both offensive and defensive magic and the ability to correctly strategize and choose between the two at a moment's notice. Players must have a crest to participate in this event.

Players are given access to their 5 spells and set on a playing field about half the size of a soccer field that has been divided in two. Each side has 12 pillars made of magically reinforced stone evenly spaced out in rows of 4 at the end of their section, with the middle section being clear of obstacles. Each row of pillars is more reinforced than the last, meaning that the pillars at the back are the harder ones to destroy.

The goal of the game is to destroy the opponents pillars while protecting your own. You are not allowed to directly hinder the other player with attacks, however spell effects such as traps, conjured spirits, rebounding spells,  and countering are perfectly fine.
#### Dominion
This game requires great team dynamics, composition, leadership, and showcases a groups adaptability to different environments.

Every year, six stages from a pool of around 40 are chosen and announced at the beginning of the tournament, giving the players only a few days to strategize. Then, the stage is chosen right when the match begins, so there is no way to know what stage will be chosen before the game starts.

There are two groups of three players, however there are no "defined" roles given. Each group is given two things, a Spirit and a Headstone. The spirit is a literal conjured spirit that travels with the players and must be protected. Players are able to assign the spirit to follow any one of them with a simple command spell. The Headstone is a large monolith that stands in their starting zone and is inscribed with runes. The team's Spirit knows the spell that will destroy their team's Headstone.

The object of the game is to steal the spirit of the opponent and have it use the spell to destroy the opponent's Headstone.

The command spell is encoded onto a person's crest, meaning they only have 4 available spells. They can choose to forgo the command spell to gain access to more spell variety, however that means they are unable to command either their own Spirit of the Spirit of the opponent.

To take control of the opponent's Spirit, you must activate the spell within 30 feet of the opposing Spirit, and you cannot already have your own Spirit following you. This is to encourage more team play and group dynamics rather than allowing a single person to take over the game.

Offensive and Defensive spells are allowed, including direct attacks against players. However, all attacks must be non-lethal. Any attack deemed lethal or too much will be called by the judges and that team will be disqualified.

#### Disc Gallery
This game is supposed to challenge players spellcasting abilities by focusing on casting speed, accuracy, and potency.

In this game, two players are placed on a podium opposite one another with an open area between them. Multi-colored discs enchanted with magic are shot from the left and right of the players and into the middle field.

The enchantment on the disc causes them to dissipate when hit with magic. There are three colors of discs which each give different points and require more potent magic to destroy:
- Blue discs are worth 1 point
- Yellow discs are worth 2 points
- Red discs are worth 3 points.

The goal is to destroy more discs than your opponent. The use of Magical Tech is allowed though it must be screened and checked  beforehand. You are not able to directly impact your opponent's play through your spells.
