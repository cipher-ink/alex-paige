## Geography

The campus is laid out as a long rectangle, ringed by forest and high stone walls on every side — walls carrying the anti-apparition wards that keep the school as hidden as it is (see [[#Defences]]). From outside, nothing about the perimeter suggests what it contains; the sanctioned way in is teleportation via [[port card]], which every enrolled student and staff member carries.

Cornwall Hall dominates the center of the grounds and is the first thing anyone sees after arriving in the main square, the ward-sanctioned point every port card is keyed to. The whole campus sits under a old, deliberate enchantment that compresses its apparent scale from a distance — every building, not just Cornwall Hall, reads as smaller than it is until you're close enough to feel it. A visitor's first real sense of the Sanctum's actual size only arrives on foot.

Open, meadow-like grounds are scattered between the buildings — grass, scattered trees, and wandering paths, closer to Central Park's Sheep Meadow than a manicured quad — used for practicing, outdoor classes, and just about everything students do outside of class. A narrow river-fed creek, about fifteen feet across and never more than chest-deep, threads the entire length of campus end to end. Entering or dirtying it is against school rules — though falling in by accident happens often enough that nobody gets in real trouble for it.

## Flora & Fauna

The creek is quietly full of life — fish, frogs, turtles — despite its size. Beyond that, most of the campus's cultivated flora (including several plants with genuine hazards attached) lives at [[The Gardens]], on the western edge of campus; see that note for detail.

## Demographics

Roughly 800 students attend at any given time, split evenly across Fourth through Seventh Year (about 200 per class) — the only years housed on this campus. Students transfer in after completing their first three years and receiving their crest at [[Sanctum Preparatory]], the Sanctum's separate lower campus.[^1]

The student body is stratified into three **Rings**. The **First Ring** holds those with the greatest individual potential — the elite of their class. The **Second Ring** is nobility and prominent families who don't quite meet First Ring caliber. The **Third Ring** is everyone unaffiliated with a recognized family — commoners, heavily looked down on by the other two Rings.

Ring placement is determined entirely by the **Book of Families** (see [[#Assets]]): if a family's crest appears in the book, their children are placed in First or Second Ring. Everyone else is Third Ring.

Unofficial slang for the Rings — banned by the school, used constantly anyway — is covered in [[#Slang]] below.

## Government

There is no dedicated magical government anywhere in the wider world — magic is handled through ordinary national and local government the same way any other domain gets its own department. The Sanctum, despite its age and prestige, is formally a school first and remains separate from any government, magical or otherwise.

Internally, Headmaster [[Cassian Vael]] and Dean of Student Affairs [[Aldric Burr]] sit atop four faculty branches:

- **Academic Affairs** — department heads over the major subject clusters (Practicum & Combat, Theory & History, Enchantment & Runics, Alchemy & Botany, General Education), each setting curriculum within their area.
- **Archives & Artifacts** — record-keeping, campus technology and artifact upkeep, and archival work (this is where [[Mr. Terrell]]'s ongoing project of digitizing the school's old records fits), with the school's **Encoders** — who apply and maintain student crests — as a specialized sub-branch.
- **Student Affairs** — Burr's own office: housing, discipline, Watcher coordination, and the faculty liaison to the student Council.
- **Grounds & Wardkeeping** — maintenance of the anti-apparition wards, the campus-wide shrink-illusion, the Garden Maze's shifting enchantments, and general upkeep; the branch most likely to actually know what's true and what's rumor about places like the Old Observatory.

A six-seat **Student Council** handles inter-Ring dispute mediation, event planning, and holds a formal (if rarely exercised) right to petition the Headmaster directly. The President is always a Seventh Year and the Vice President a Sixth Year; while nothing requires it, the VP becoming President the following year has held as an unbroken norm for decades. The remaining four seats are meant to be split across the Rings — the Council's own governing document requires at least one Third Ring seat — but no Third Ring student has held a seat in over thirty years, a fact nobody currently on staff or Council has ever formally addressed.

Watchers — faculty and upper-year students who patrol the grounds, especially at night, to keep the campus in line after curfew — are a separate, informal duty from the Council. Membership can overlap, but being one doesn't imply the other.

## Defences

The campus walls carry an anti-apparition ward: nobody can teleport in or out without a **port card** — a physical, ID-card-style token that doubles as a student's identification. Temporary passes can be issued for parents and other guests. Scrying mirrors are posted at key chokepoints (the gatehouse, dorm entrances) so faculty can monitor them remotely, the school's answer to a security camera.

There's no dedicated police force. Watchers and faculty — all of them trained magi — handle enforcement directly. There are no formal jail cells or detention halls, though the basement rooms of Cornwall Hall have historically been pressed into service for holding people when needed. **Mana-cuffs** are used when necessary, suppressing a wearer's ability to channel magic entirely.

The wards aren't absolute. A hidden underground passage runs beneath the outer wall, surfacing on a hillside outside campus — known to a few, not documented anywhere official. A handful of ordinary-looking objects scattered around the school are, unknown to nearly everyone, functioning portkeys. There's also a fixed, cardless portal hidden in a cliff face in the surrounding forest, roughly one to two hours from [[Cabin|Alex's family cabin]] — a genuine secret entrance rather than a second "main" one, known to [[Mr. Terrell]] among others. It's how Alex herself first gets onto campus, using a key Terrell gives her at her father's request. The creek itself was sealed against use as an escape route once the school realized how obvious an exit it made.

## Infrastructure

The creek is the campus's main piece of natural infrastructure, its waters diverted in places for irrigation at [[The Gardens]]. Paths connect the scattered meadow spaces to each academic and residential building. The sanctioned way onto campus is teleportation via port card, arriving directly in the main square outside Cornwall Hall — see [[#Defences]] for the handful of secret alternatives.

## Assets

The **Book of Families** is kept on proud display in Cornwall Hall's front foyer when it isn't in active use. It holds the crest designs of every prominent family the school has ever come into contact with, and it's the sole determinant of a student's Ring (see [[#Demographics]]).

## History

The Sanctum was founded in 1355 by [[Allya Holden]], explicitly to preserve her father's work and guard against another catastrophe on the scale of the Crusade that claimed him. Cornwall Hall, the campus's oldest structure, dates to the founding itself. Its first headmaster, [[Alaeric Cornwall]], gave the school its guiding creed — *"To know Truth, fundamentally, is to know all"* — which the school still builds its curriculum around, if not always in the spirit he intended.

[[Voss Observatory]] is a more recent addition, named for an alum credited with discovering that certain star alignments measurably affect leyline potency around the Magic Solstice — research that, among other things, is the reason the [[Intra-School Tournament]] is timed to the Solstice every four years. It replaced an older observatory building, still standing but abandoned and off-limits, tucked into a quiet corner of campus and mostly known now for rumors that it's haunted.

## Architecture

Cornwall Hall sets the tone for the whole campus: gothic, multiple stories tall, roughly seven stories with a long central tower, comparable in scale to Notre-Dame — though its rectangular footprint (longer than it is wide) means it doesn't fully register as that large until a visitor is standing close to it, thanks to the campus-wide shrink-illusion. It houses the Great Hall, the library, administrative offices, and traditional classrooms.

Helios A and Helios B are curved, matched academic buildings that wrap around a shared circular courtyard, at the center of which floats the **Helios Forma** — a thirty-foot bronze sphere etched with ancient runic seals nobody has fully deciphered. Decoding its inscriptions has become something of a rite of passage, especially for students pursuing enchanting.

The three dormitories — [[Dominus Hall]], [[Eskavar Hall]], and [[Charter Hall]] — are each distinct in scale, material, and upkeep, reflecting the Ring hierarchy in physical form.

## Sensory & Seasonal Variation

The campus feels different across the school year. The fall opening is ceremonial and crisp — the return and sorting of students into their years. Spring brings the grounds to their fullest, coinciding with the Fifth Year Advancement Tournament and the Trials. Winter break empties the campus out almost entirely, leaving it noticeably quiet — the students who remain are disproportionately Third Ring students without homes worth returning to, a detail the school doesn't advertise and nobody really discusses.

## Curriculum & Academics

The curriculum is built around a quote from the school's first headmaster, [[Alaeric Cornwall]]:

> "To know Truth, fundamentally, is to know all."

The phrase has been taken out of context more than once, but within the school it's generally understood to mean that a foundational understanding of magic and how it works is the key to wielding it well.

All students also take generalized coursework — reading, writing, math, history, science — to keep pace with normal educational standards, though most of it carries a magical "spin."

### First Years[^1]
Taught mostly theory rather than practical application — considered too young and immature to handle magic unsupervised, and most haven't yet awakened a manifestation.
- **Classes:** Magic History, Basic Magical Theory, Creatures and Beings, Botany, Astronomy, Mathematics

### Second Years[^1]
Begin practical spellcasting — learning to manipulate the weave for small, controlled effects. Students acquire a focus to help channel magic, discover their affinity, and are given a Grimoire.
- **Classes:** Ancient Practicum (spellcasting), Alchemy, Attunements, Ecology, Enchantment Theory (Magic Tech), Grimoires

### Third Years[^1]
More advanced coursework begins, alongside early study of manifestations and basic crest theory ahead of receiving a crest the following year. This is also the first year students can join a team and compete in the Intra-School Competition.
- **Classes:** Ritual Magic, Runics, Archeology, Basic Defensive Magics, Conjuration, Manifestations, Crest Theory

### Fourth Years
Students acquire their crest and begin modern spellcasting instruction, along with the basics of dueling and offensive magic. This is also when students transfer onto the main Sanctum campus.
- **Classes:** Spell Structure, Advanced Magic Theory, Spell Mathematics, Dueling, Special Electives, Cosmology

### Fifth Years
Eligible to compete in the fall Advancement Tournament. Students are expected to form groups of three or four and register with the school for their remaining years — these groups become essential going forward.
- **Classes:** Magic Ethics and Law, Advanced Practicum, Enchantment Practicum (magic tech creation), Contracts

### Sixth Years
Students become eligible for Quests — independent studies that send them out into the world (fetch quests, internships, study-abroad programs) alongside their registered group. Fewer classes from here on, but more responsibility.
- **Classes:** Quests (Independent Study), Magic Culture, Group Dynamics (leadership)

### Seventh Years
The final year. Only two formal classes remain; the bulk of the year goes toward a thesis project developed and presented with the student's group. Some theses have gone on to meaningfully influence the wider magical world.
- **Classes:** Thesis, Advanced Dueling, Layman Studies

## Slang

Owing to the school's age, magic users here are still called **Magi** rather than the more modern term Operators.

Unofficial (and officially banned) terms exist for the Rings, used by students the moment faculty are out of earshot. **"Blooms"** refers to First and Second Ring students — the implication being beauty, cultivation, and a kind of natural selection where only the "most fit" belong. **"Weeds"** refers to Third Ring students — unwanted, unworthy, the implicit trash of the student body in the eyes of many First and Second Ring families.
