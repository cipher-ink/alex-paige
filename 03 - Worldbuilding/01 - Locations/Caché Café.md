# Caché Café
**Also Called:** The Hidden Mug, The Maze-End, Emeer’s Drudge (derogatory, used by older faculty)
**Location:** Center of the Garden Maze, eastern edge of the Sanctum campus
**Hours:** Six days a week, closed Sundays. Opens at dusk and closes when the last student finds their way out—or when Professor [[Albricht Emeer]] decides the till is counted.

---

## Description
The center of the maze is not what the dark green hedges would suggest. Where students would expect a dense growth, the ground rises in a single, deliberate terrace–flattened, level, and open to the sky. You cannot see directly over the maze walls; the hedges stand just tall enough to frame the view of the sky like the edges of a painting. But the sky above is wide and unobstructed. There is a barrier as well that prevents weather from affecting this area, so it’s a safe space for getting away from the rain.

From the outside, the terrace doens’t exist. An old but still working drift ward keeps the space indistinct to anyone peering in from above, seeming like the maze just continues towards the center. It’s not a proper enchantment, simply because it’s always worked and Professor Emeer doesn’t want to pay to replace it. Though the  maze is hidden and there are no signs, it’s probably the worst kept secret on the campus. Faculty even mention it during orientation. But the mystery still persists if not in fact, then the cafe’s name.
#### The Terrace
The terrace itself is a broad rectangle of pale stone, swept clean each morning. It’s edges are lined with potted plants in no particular order – ferns spilling over ceramic rims, late-season flowers in bright clusters, a few specimans that are magical just for fun. The arrangement shifts with the seasons and depending on what Professor Linden has spare from the First Terrace. The combined effect is a space that always smells floral with the deep bitterness of coffee drifting from the booth.
#### The Booth
The booth sits near the back of the terrace, small and sharp-angled, more a kiosk than a building. It’s matte black paneling and flat roof makes it look much to modern for such an old school, as if dropped here by mistake. The contrast is jarring in a way that most students find charming next to the centuries old hedge walls.

Inside there is room for three students at most, four if they really like touching shoulders. A narrow counter separates the customer space from the compact kitchenette: dual boilers, a small espresso unit, a warming plate for milk, and a low cabinet of supplies. The walls are a magnetic steel covered with overlapping notes, schedules, and even some food-safe work posters. Even if they are magic, they still stay up to code.

On the outside face, a large chalkboard hands from the awning. It seems to rewrite itself as new things are added to the menu every few days: the standard offerings, seasonal specials written in different colored chalks, and occasional warnings for when things are out. Below the board is a narrow stand that holds more accessories. Cream, sugar, honey, straws, and the like.

## Atmosphere
Because the maze is technically unregulated common ground, the Caché Café is one of the few places on campus where Ring divisions are *technically* set aside. First-Ring students sit beside Third-Ring students at the same tables, though the seating tends to sort itself: Blooms near the far end and Weeds closer to the booth. The pretense of equality is maintained by mutual unspoken agreement. No one mentions Rings here. It is not forbidden; it is simply *tacky*.

Professor Emeer insists the café operate on a sliding scale: full price for those who can pay, half-price for those who cannot, and a discreet IOU system for Third-Ring students in their first semester. The IOUs are rarely collected. It is widely understood that Emeer burns them at the end of every term in the Aegon Hall hearth.

## Menu
The Caché Café rarely ever serves food except for special occasions. The offerings are limited, student-priced, and heavily caffeinated:
- **Standard offerings:** Black tea, chamomile (sometimes laced with dried [[Heartsease]] for anxious seventh-years), bitter cocoa, and a fiercely strong coffee known as "Linden Brew".
- **Seasonal:** Iced [[Glasslily]] water in summer; warm honeyed mulled wine (non-alcoholic) in winter.
- **Signature:** The Frênedoré, a caramel flavored milk drink with ash-like foam on the top.

### The Frênedoré
*Pronounced [fren-doh-rey], Meaning “Golden Ash” in French*
*Most commonly called a “Fray” by  students.*
Served typically in a thuck ceramic mug, it has a heavy gray cap of fine, ash-like foam that sits atop warm, milky liquid the color of dark caramel. First-timers often pause. It looks like someone served you hot milk from a swept fireplace. The foam is dense and matte and clings to the lip of the mug as well as the person drinking it.

The foam gives way and is in reality light and clean, with a bit of sweetness but not much flavor on it’s own. It’s the texture and theatre more than taste. What it underneath is warm, rich, and tastes much like caramel but with a strange second note that arrives after the sweetness: floral, faintly honeyed, something between an orange and crushed vanilla leaf. That’s the Asphetal.

Drinking it feels like a warmth slowly spreading through your body, and when mixed with the glasslilly water in the iced version, a sense of cool breeze. There is no magical effect to drinking it, but some students say that it’s great to drink before an exam to steady the hands.
##### Variants
- **Clean Fray:** The asphetal syrup is left out and it’s simply the milk and foam. For those who don’t like the taste of the syrup of simply want less calories than the original. Those who want to mimic the taste often put caramel syrup inside, though it’s not exactly the same.
- **Dirty Fray:** The same drink mixed with espresso for a caffine kick that overpowers the original floral notes and combines with the caramel to give a more “bitter-sweet” taste.
- **London Fray:** Mixed with black or green tea, it retains and even brings out the more floral tastes while mellowing out the richer caramel flavor. The cafe’s take on a london fog.
- **Chai Fray:** The syrup is replaced entirely with steamed chai with just the foam on top. Not really a variant, but was given the same moniker nonetheless.
- **Iced Fray:** Mixed with glasslilly water (which is not really water but just the extract of the lilly), this iced drink turns the warm feeling of drinking the Fray to a soothing chill that is great for hot summer days.
- **Frappe Fray:** Any of the above, with the exception of the London, can be turned into a frappe at the request of the customer.

##### Ingredients
**Asphetal Syrup:** The heart of the drink. A syrup made from asphetal leaves–the same visd orange-red blooms hidden beneath a gray soot dust that can be seen along the paths of the Sanctum as well as in the gardens. The leaves are mulled gently in water to soften them, then ground and pureed into a thin amber paste. The paste is forced into a reduction of campus honey and browned butter to create a thick syrup the color of burnt sugar.

It’s taste is naturally sweet and faintly perfumed, like a honeysuckle but more earthy and a low warmth that lingers on the tongue. When mixed into steamed milk, it produces a flavor profile that students love yet struggle to describe. “Like drinking a candle flame”. The closes description that most people agree on has come from Professor Linden herself, describing it as “what warm light would taste like if it had calories”.

**The Foam:** The signature grey cap is made from asphetal dust–the fine mineral secretion that coats the blooms and regenerates within hours of being brushed. Harvested fresh from the first terrace each morning, the dust is whisked into warmed cream with a measured spoon of sugar. The dust itself tastes of very little: mineral, slightly chalky. The sugar rounds it into something pleasent and neutral. The result is a stable and dense foam the color of cold ash, sweet enough to stand alone but is designed to be tasted with other drinks.

As the foam melts into the drink, the grey briefly streaks in the liquid with swirling patterns like smoke through honey before settling and ultimately dissolving completely over time.

## Faculty Relations
Professor [[Albricht Emeer]] sponsors the café personally, using a portion of his Ethics salary to cover ingredient costs and equipment. He claims it is a "practical lesson in equitable resource distribution." His colleagues in the administration find it unseemly. His students find it the only proof that anyone in authority actually cares.

Professor [[Linden]] (Botany) supplies several of the café’s botanical ingredients directly from the [[The Gardens|First Terrace]], including Heartsease, Glasslily trimmings, and—since it was discovered—fresh [[Asphetal]] for the Fray. She takes payment in free chamomile and the occasional favor.

## Secret / Rumored Details
- **The Ledger:** Emeer keeps a handwritten ledger of every IOU he has ever issued. The handwriting in the margins is not his.
- **The Back Door:** There are rumors of a hidden entracne to the cafe that the student workers and Emeer himself use to get to the place without needing to navigate the ever-changing maze. Professor Linden may also use it for deliveries of ingredients, though no student has ever found it.
- **The Echoweed Bench:** A stone seat just outside the café door, overgrown with Echoweed. On nights when the weed is loud, couples avoid it. On quiet nights, it is the most popular spot on campus for romantic confessions whether on purpose or not.
