# The Gardens

**Also Called:** Linden’s Terraces, The Weeding Grounds (student slang)

**Location:** Western edge of the Sanctum campus, built into a gradual hillside just south of Charter Hall and buffered from the warded forest by a low stone wall.

---

## Layout

The gardens are arranged as **four tiered stone terraces** cut into the hillside, connected by a central stair of weathered granite. A narrow river-channel diverted from the main campus waterway threads along the base, mainly used for irrigation and runoff control. Each terrace has its own microclimate and purpose, and students quickly learn that the higher you go, the less forgiving the flora becomes.

### First Terrace — The Commons
The ground-level plot where introductory herbology classes work and where detention labor is assigned. This terrace grows "safe" medicinal stock: Heartsease, standard chamomile and mint varietals, and Asphetal planted along the central path as a decorative border. Even here, "safe" is relative—Echoweed invades every bed no matter how often it is pulled.

A tool shed and wash station sit at the north end, along with a covered potting bench where Professor Linden keeps her field journals. The soil here is dark and loamy, heavily enriched by compost from the Aegon Hall kitchens. Glasslilies grow in patches along the eastern retaining wall where they catch moonlight.

### Second Terrace — The Middle Beds
Intermediate-strength flora grown for alchemy and potion-making classes. This level requires gloves and basic protective charms. Rows are narrower and separated by gravel channels to contain aggressive root systems.

### Third Terrace — The Vigil Beds
Where the garden’s more aggressive specimens live in reinforced planters and warded enclosures. This is where mature Bitermoss is cultivated in broad sealed troughs, and where plants with mild psychic properties are kept away from sensitive minds. A faint earthy ozone smell lingers here from containment runes carved into the retaining walls.

### Fourth Terrace — The High Plot
Professor Linden’s private research space and experimental greenhouse. Students rarely go here unsupervised. Rumor says she keeps a few specimens that predate the Sanctum itself, and that the Echoweed whispers are loudest at this elevation. The stair to the fourth terrace is technically unlocked, but most students find reasons not to climb that high.

---

## Atmosphere

At night the gardens are lit only by Glasslilies and whatever moonlight makes it past the campus wards. The sound of the irrigation channel provides a constant low murmur, occasionally interrupted by the snap of Bitermoss settling after a feeding. The Echoweed grows thickest along the eastern retaining wall where residual spellcast from nearby classrooms leeches into the soil.

Because of the Echoweed, some students claim the gardens are the most truthfully observed place on campus—nothing here stays hidden for long.

---

## Notable Features

- **The Compost Heap:** Located beyond the south wall. Unofficially where failed enchantments and expired reagents are dumped, which may explain why the local Echoweed is unusually potent.
- **Linden’s Wards:** Carved sigils along each retaining wall that dampen the more dangerous properties of the plants within the terrace boundaries. Without them, the third terrace would be a minor hazard zone.
- **The Hidden Bench:** A stone seat tucked behind a Glasslily patch on the first terrace, mostly obscured by growth. Students use it to hide from patrols or eavesdrop on the Echoweed.

## Flora

### Bitermoss
**Location:** Tier 3 — Vigil Beds, kept in sealed stone troughs behind reinforced wards.

A carnivorous moss with a dense, creeping green base. Scattered across its surface are small white flowers about the size of a quarter, faintly luminous and giving off a sweet, nectar-like scent to attract prey. Mixed invisibly through the green are thousands of trigger hairs. Disturb them and the moss releases a cloud of paralyzing spores. The paralysis takes a few seconds to set in; strong enough to knock out a small child for hours without intervention, while most teenagers and adults can feel the numbness creeping in and stumble clear before it fully takes hold.

Once the prey is down, the moss senses body heat and grows toward it, seeking orifices to thread itself into. Suffocation takes hours. It is exactly as horrible as it sounds, which is why it is container-grown and sealed on Tier 3. Only sixth and seventh years handle it for thesis research under direct supervision.

Linden goes up to feed it once a week—chunks of raw meat from the Aegon Hall kitchens that she tosses in like she is feeding koi. Students on Tier 1 can hear the moss shift and settle when it feeds, even if they cannot see it.

### Glasslily
**Location:** Tier 1 — Commons, along the eastern retaining wall.

Almost entirely translucent, with petals like thin glass or frozen water. During the day they are nearly invisible against the soil, but after sunset they give off a soft, steady glow—cool white, not warm. They feed on sunlight the way ordinary plants do, storing it through the daylight hours. Students who serve evening detention work by lily-light. The glow is gentle enough that it does not ruin night vision, which is useful when trying not to trip over a trowel in the dark.

### Heartsease
**Location:** Tier 1 — Commons, scattered in the medicinal beds.

A humble leafy herb with faint silver veining. Chewing a leaf slows the heart rate and calms the nerves. Linden cultivates it for the infirmary—useful for students coming down from panic attacks or manifestation overload. She keeps dried leaves in her apron pocket and hands them out to anxious kids without much ceremony. The taste is grassy and slightly bitter, like strong green tea left out too long.

### Bramère
**Location:** Tier 1 — Commons, scattered through the medicinal beds and soil paths.

A low-growing herb with thick, bulbous pods that sit just above the soil line, mottled brown and green so they resemble river stones half-buried in the dirt. Inside each pod is a fibrous stalk lined with tiny ridged barbs. Disturb one—step too close, kneel beside it, let a sleeve brush over it—and the pod lunges upward and clamps shut with surprising force. The barbs are soft and the pressure is all mechanical; it cannot break skin through fabric and it carries no venom or spores. But a direct pinch on bare skin leaves a nasty welt and bruise, and the shock is enough to make even experienced gardeners swear.

Students who have worked the Commons learn to spot the slight domed rise in the soil where a Bramère lurks. Newcomers find out the hard way.

### Asphetal
**Location:** Tier 1 — Commons, decorative border along the central path.

The newest addition to the gardens, discovered less than five years ago and planted as a decorative border just last spring. It looks like it is covered in fine gray soot or asphalt dust. The "ash" is actually a mineral-rich secretion that acts as a natural fire retardant. Touch the bloom and the dust falls away, revealing vivid orange-red petals underneath. Within a few hours the dust regenerates. Linden keeps a row of them near the wash station because they are hardy, eye-catching, and students cannot resist brushing the dust off to see the color beneath. It gives them something harmless to fidget with while she explains the rules.
